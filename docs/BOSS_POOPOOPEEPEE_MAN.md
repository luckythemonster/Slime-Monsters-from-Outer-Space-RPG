# Boss: the poopoopeepee man

Status: the boss, both names, his mission statement, who he is a caricature of, the scat joke, the placement, the list (that it gets worse, that it can be cut off, that it has one Beatle in it, and where it ends), how he holds the two meanings, his brother Earl, the rotating backing band, and that the rotating band is seen on screen are *(canon, Lucky)*. Mechanics, the rewards, the order of the middle of the list, the staging of the second appearance, and every line of dialogue except the mission statement are proposal.

## One line

A shock-rock frontman who wants to "bring poopoo and peepee back to rock and roll." Asked which rock and roll greats of days gone by were into scat, he names a jazz singer. He is not joking.

## Who he is

- **His name.** *(canon, Lucky)* His first name is "the poopoopeepee." His last name is Man. Nameplate: THE POOPOOPEEPEE MAN. Lowercase when anyone says it out loud.
- **What he is:** a caricature of GG Allin as a type: the frontman whose whole act is the threat of what he is about to do to the room. *(canon, Lucky)*
- **Art note:** the sprite is an original design and not a likeness. The real name appears in the game exactly once, as the last name on his list.
- **The two meanings.** *(canon, Lucky)* For him they are interchangeable. Scat is scat. He does not understand what poop has to do with vocal improvisation in that style, and he goes with it anyway. He is not confused and he is not doing a bit. Somebody handed him one word and he has been faithful to all of it. Nobody corrects him, because there is nothing to correct. Add him to "Never explained."

## His band

- **Earl Man.** *(canon, Lucky)* His brother. Plays bass for him. Always there, every appearance. Nameplate: EARL MAN. He has heard the list before. Their parents named one son Earl and the other one the poopoopeepee, and the game does not go into it.
- **Everyone else.** *(canon, Lucky)* Every time the poopoopeepee man appears, the rest of the backing band is different people. Every band gets grossed out at the first show and quits. The game never shows what grossed them out.
- **The gag is on screen.** *(canon, Lucky)* He appears twice so the player sees it happen: two lineups, one Earl, and both lineups gone by the time the band looks back.

## First appearance: Chapter 4, the opening round

**South by South-Wasteland, the Side Stage, the opening round of the bracket.** *(canon, Lucky)* He is the band the Slime Monsters have to get past before Alibi.

- Chapter 4 is already about tacky against craft. The Tall Guitarist's verdict on Alibi is "It's tacky. And it's boring." The poopoopeepee man is tacky and never boring, which sets the scale before the judges use it.
- Chapter 4 is a chapter about grids: Alibi on 4/4, Sticky on 13/8. He adds a third: swing.
- The bracket board already has a slot for him, next to KURT BREEZESTEIN, DISQUALIFIED (EXCESSIVE TONNAGE).
- It needs no new system beyond a late timing window. It reuses the beat-timed input from Chapter 2 and TALK from Chapter 3.

## The list

*(canon, Lucky: the list gets progressively worse, the player can cut it off at any point, one name on it is the Beatle who did the scatting on "Come Together," and it ends on "GG Allin.")*

After the first answer the prompt is **[Keep asking] / [That's enough]**. Each [Keep asking] gets one more name, delivered with the same reverence as the first. [That's enough] at any point ends it and nothing further is lost. Most players will stop early. The rest is an easter egg that gets worse the deeper it goes.

The order (proposal, except the first and the last):

1. "Ella Fitzgerald."
2. "Louis Armstrong."
3. "Cab Calloway."
4. "John Lennon." Diego: "Okay. That one's rock and roll, man." THE POOPOOPEEPEE MAN: "They all are. 'Come Together.'" It is the one time on the whole list that he answers the question he was asked. Nobody notices, including him.
5. "Scatman Crothers." Earl looks at the floor.
6. "Scatman John." Phoenix: "My brain thinks he's just reading names with the word in them now."
7. "GG Allin."

The last one is the only name on the list that means the other thing. He does not mark the difference.

> Diego: "That one's not a jazz singer, man."
>
> THE POOPOOPEEPEE MAN: "Same thing."

Earl, who has not spoken all fight: "Yep, that's the list."

## The fight: "Bring It Back"

**The rule: the bar is the only filthy thing in the fight.** Nothing gross ever happens on screen. The whole fight is the threat.

- **IMMINENT.** His bar is not HP. It is labeled IMMINENT and it fills every round as he announces, in detail-free terms, what is about to happen to the first three rows.
- **The room reacts.** The CROWD meter drains as the crowd backs up. Ponchos and umbrellas open in the front row as objects. The Tall Guitarist slides his chair back one tile per round. So do the guitarist and the drummer.
- **At 100%.** He takes the biggest breath in the game. The screen holds. Then he scats. Flawless, fast bebop: SHOO-BE-DOO, BIP, ZAZ, SKEE-BOP as objects, thrown at the party on swung eighths. As far as he is concerned this is the thing he promised.
- **Swing.** Clean on-the-beat inputs miss. The dodge window sits late, on the back third of the beat. The player learns to play late on purpose here, which is what Alibi has to learn in Chapter 10.
- **Earl.** Holds the root under all of it and never drops a bar. He is the only fixed point in the fight, so his bass line is the tell for where the swing sits.
- **Sticky.** Sticky cannot swing either. Its 13/8 lands inside his phrases at random and knocks syllables out of the air. The defect is the party's best defense.
- **TALK.** After the first chorus Diego's TALK gains one entry: "Which greats?" It plays the joke. He is so pleased somebody finally asked that he loses his place in the form, and IMMINENT empties.
- **The win.** Every name on the list costs him a turn. [That's enough] or the end of the list brings the judges' buzzer. He leaves happy.

**Bracket board afterward:** THE POOPOOPEEPEE MAN, DISQUALIFIED (SANITATION). He never did anything.

**Rewards:** PONCHO (WEAR), taken from the front row. It was never needed. Hearing the whole list also gets a sticker: ONE OF THE GREATS.

**Crowd bank:** the crowd that backed away comes back for the Slime Monsters' set, so the round banks more than it drained.

## Second appearance: Chapter 10, Palmer's II

No fight. Chapter 10 already has two bosses and the rider. This is the gag landing. *(canon, Lucky: he appears again, same Earl, new band; staging is proposal)*

- **The booking.** He is the house act at the Vanguard's "Authentic Dive Experience." Brian booked him as a pre-approved amount of danger. The marquee reads TONIGHT: THE P. MAN. A laminated card taped to his monitor reads IMMINENT: 0% (PER MANAGEMENT).
- **The lineup.** Earl is on bass. The guitarist and the drummer are two people nobody has seen before, in price-tagged leather jackets, the same wardrobe department as the cop in Chapter 1.
- **The old lineup.** The two session players from Chapter 4 work the gift-shop register now. When he walks past they look at the floor. Talk to them: "We don't do live work anymore."
- **Him.** He is not bitter about the card. He sees Diego across the room: "You asked." If the player heard the whole list in Chapter 4, he adds: "All seven."
- **Phoenix:** "My brain thinks those are different guys." Earl: "Yep."
- **The payoff.** When the band comes back through Palmer's II from the north woods, the stage has Earl, two instruments on stands, and a flyer on the corkboard: HELP WANTED. GUITAR, DRUMS. STRONG STOMACH. Nobody says what happened. The card on the monitor still reads 0%.
