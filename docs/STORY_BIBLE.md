## Voice rules

- Phoenix prefixes every thought with "My brain thinks…" and means it literally. The one time they stop (Cyan) is the scariest thing in the game; the first time they aim it at someone else (Dad) is the bravest. The last line of the game is the first time they say "I think." Cyan was the brain with nobody in it; this is the person owning the brain. *(canon, Lucky)*
- Ryan's captions are rigid square boxes, the calmest text in the loudest room. "Yep." is a complete sentence. After Chapter 1 he has no lines and one caption left (Ch 10, from behind a fence); Lucky gets to say "Yep." once *(canon, Lucky)*.
- Diego says "man," reads auras, holds space, and is never wrong about vibes, only about everything else. When he cannot read someone at all, he says so and goes. *(canon, Lucky)*
- David talks like a sound guy: check, levels, less of everything. He has never asked anyone to turn down who did not need to. *(canon, Lucky; lines are proposal)*
- Bajonka never speaks as a dog and is never explained. For one night in Chapter 10 she is human and speaks: short, itemized, statements only *(canon, Lucky)*. Lucky's color is the HUD; magenta hits harder and worse.
- Lucky counts everything in: "One, two, three, four!" From Disc 2 they call the changes too (CALL THE CHANGE). Nobody in the band fines anybody. A missed cue goes on Bajonka's clipboard: MISSED CUE, $5. *(canon, Lucky)*
- Villains speak corporate; the band speaks gig. Nobody says "evil." Brian is never a villain in his own mind. Brian is a brain in a jar and nobody ever mentions it: no character says so, the word never appears in dialogue, and he never learns. His portrait is a normal guy, which is how he sees himself. His sprite is the jar. *(canon, Lucky)*
- Sound effects are objects. Every chapter ends broke; if a chapter ends with money, it is a countdown.
- Toon Level: 0 static, 1 shake, 2 SFX letters become objects, 3 the UI frame tears, 4 the art style ruptures.

## Midpoint design

Planets Collide is the hinge because it is the only fight the band's noise cannot win, and it takes away everything Disc 1 accumulated at once: the apartment, the van, the coupon, the planet, and the laser guitar.

Disc 1 teaches one rule five times: the band's noise breaks rooms. Chapter 6 offers a sixth room, the sky, and the player cannot move the bar. The bosses framing Disc 1 are the same fight from opposite sides: the Palmer's set *cannot be lost* (the only choice was whether to let go, and it fired either way); the Sludge Duel *cannot be won* (every input works, nothing matters). INTERMISSION sits between them — the game's one honest lie: the HUD never fakes a number, but the bar is labeled GRAVITY and the game-over card is a fake.

What flips: home (eviction → no planet); the instrument (locked → gone → Lucky's voice); Phoenix (bass → synth → Greg's bass); the Vanguard (khakis → the band is wanted); the rhythm section (Ryan ran and nobody replaced him; the drummer the band keeps is the one who can't play 4/4, and Lucky learns to count him in) *(canon, Lucky)*; Lucky (reactive magenta → leading by choice).

Balancing the halves: Disc 1 compresses nine front-loaded scripts into six chapters, each turning on a relationship; Disc 2 builds six from the Grand Finale's five parts, each fused to a thread the comic only seeded. Disc 1 is losing people. Disc 2 is finding out who comes back. Nobody does. The one who was there the whole time is a dog. *(canon, Lucky)* About nine hours each.

## The Chapter 9 rescue (Bajonka)

**What happens.** The literal Death Star: the ship Lucky escaped from, come back for them.
