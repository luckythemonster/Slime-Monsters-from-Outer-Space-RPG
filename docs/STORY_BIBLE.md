## Voice rules

- Phoenix prefixes every thought with "My brain thinks…" and means it literally. The one time they stop (Cyan) is the scariest thing in the game; the first time they aim it at someone else (Dad) is the bravest.
- Ryan's captions are rigid square boxes, the calmest text in the loudest room. "Yep." is a complete sentence.
- Diego says "man," reads auras, holds space, and is never wrong about vibes, only about everything else.
- Bajonka never speaks and is never explained. Lucky's color is the HUD; magenta hits harder and worse.
- Villains speak corporate; the band speaks gig. Nobody says "evil." Brian is never a villain in his own mind.
- Sound effects are objects. Every chapter ends broke; if a chapter ends with money, it is a countdown.
- Toon Level: 0 static, 1 shake, 2 SFX letters become objects, 3 the UI frame tears, 4 the art style ruptures.

## Midpoint design

Planets Collide is the hinge because it is the only fight the band's noise cannot win, and it takes away everything Disc 1 accumulated at once: the apartment, the van, the coupon, the planet, and the laser guitar.

Disc 1 teaches one rule five times: the band's noise breaks rooms. Chapter 6 offers a sixth room, the sky, and the player cannot move the bar. The bosses framing Disc 1 are the same fight from opposite sides: the Palmer's set *cannot be lost* (the only input was LET GO); the Sludge Duel *cannot be won* (every input works, nothing matters). INTERMISSION sits between them — the game's one honest lie: the HUD never fakes a number, but the bar is labeled GRAVITY and the game-over card is a fake.

What flips: home (eviction → no planet); the instrument (locked → gone → Lucky's voice); Phoenix (bass → synth; puddle → choosing Cyan); the Vanguard (khakis → the band is wanted); the rhythm section (Ryan ran → Ryan comes back); Lucky (reactive magenta → leading by choice).

Balancing the halves: Disc 1 compresses nine front-loaded scripts into six chapters, each turning on a relationship; Disc 2 builds six from the Grand Finale's five parts, each fused to a thread the comic only seeded. Disc 1 is losing people. Disc 2 is going back for them. About nine hours each.

## Billy Ruben

**Who.** A bilirubin-amber slime — jaundice yellow; the joke is in the name — bassist and wall-of-sound engineer, now pirate DJ of Radio Free California inside a hollowed-out WORK IS LOVE billboard. They/them. Billy holds walls of sound; Lucky writes the songs that stand on them. Billy wrote the old band's only rule — **NO GUITAR SOLO NECESSARY** — which is why Bajonka's flyer stops Lucky cold in Chapter 1.

**Backstory.** Lucky and Billy were **NO DENTAL**, two teenage slimes in a Planet California storage unit, named for the one thing Dad kept offering. Dad shut the unit down and had their biggest show raided; Lucky was dragged onto the star-cruiser, and the pod bay on screen one is where that night ends. There were two pods. Lucky hit EJECT without checking the next one. Billy walked into the bay as the pod fired and watched the contrail through the glass. Lucky has spent the whole story telling themself Billy didn't show. Billy didn't chase: kept the amp, recorded a tape, built a barge out of a billboard, and when Lucky "asked the universe" in Chapter 1 the wave reached Planet California. Billy has followed its echoes one gig behind ever since.

**The Han Solo moment (Chapter 9, beat 10).** The literal Death Star: the ship Lucky escaped from, come back for them. Tractor beam, power cut, commands greying out. Then every billboard plays the NO DENTAL riff at once; a billboard-barge drops out of hyperspace and rakes the beam emitter with a wall of sound; the beam stutters; *the player* takes the shot. "You left the amp on, dude. I could hear you from here." Billy joins as a guest. Command: **WALL OF SOUND** — a sustain that holds a battle open for a round; it is what lets Lucky put the guitar down in Chapter 12.

**Why there.** (1) Chapter 9 asks whether Lucky's old life was real, and Billy is the answer: a bandmate who stayed and never held the leaving against them. (2) Billy forgives Lucky for leaving so Lucky can forgive Ryan in Chapter 10; Lucky's rage at Ryan was always partly guilt about Billy. (3) Han clears the shot; he does not take it — the rescue rewards growth instead of replacing it. (4) At the final boss the arrival would compete with the fourth-wall break; here it is the climax of its own chapter.

## Ending variants

All three keep the direction Lucky likes — the staged suicide, Trunk, the paywall, the golden toilet, the tweet-birds, the fourth-wall break, still broke because of Artisanal Anti-Matter — and all end in the gold cockpit on "My brain thinks... my brain thinks it's a hundred thousand credits per gallon." / "We better sell some merch at this next one, man."

**V1 — Roll Him Up (closest to the comic; recommended, with V3's banked damage underneath).** Lucky's critique physically breaks the game. Trunk's 16-color sprite shatters; behind it, on flat white, is a deliberately *wrong* asset — a hyper-realistic, Dadaist photo-collage at the wrong resolution, breaking the game's palette rule. The command menu corrupts into a gray OS dialog with one button. *Mechanics:* the one input is **ROLL** — Lucky's cartoon hand becomes the cursor and the player drags it to roll the cutouts into a massive paper joint; Billy's Wall of Sound holds the battle open until the drag completes. Lucky lights it off the plasma-jet strings and exhales pink smoke. *Last beat:* the cockpit, the fuel gauge, Diego unplugging the talk radio, Lucky plugging in, the next gig.

**V2 — Allegory: OFF.** Lucky's complaint opens the game's own Options menu over the fight. A new toggle sits at the bottom — ALLEGORY [ON] — in a row that has been empty all game. OFF strips Trunk to a magenta-and-black checkerboard placeholder labeled RICH GUY (TEMP). Lucky rolls the placeholder and smokes it. *Mechanics:* Phase 3 is a menu puzzle — leave the battle UI, find the toggle, flip it while the birds keep screaming; nothing can hurt the party during the menu, which is the point. *Last beat:* the checkerboard cube bungeed to the hood; Artisanal Anti-Matter; Diego: "Honestly the placeholder had a better aura."

**V3 — The Scene.** Lucky turns from the camera to the crowd — and Billy's barge is a clown car: the cyborg farmers, the Terrifying Librarian (one SHHHH deletes every bird), Greg, Kevin, the Tall Guitarist's finger, Warner, Shannon, Little Guy. ## Running gags, motifs, themes

- **Bajonka handled it.** Clipboard → contract → flyer → van keys → dock guard → Denim Boys → claymore → dossier → escape pod → prison break. Each stinger she appears in is the next chapter's premise. Her one blind spot is Pete.
- **Every chapter ends broke.** The pothole bill, four dollars, the eviction, the deposit, the fuel gauge. The economy is the antagonist; Trunk is its face. The **Pothole Invoice** rides in the inventory to the credits.
- **"NO GUITAR SOLO NECESSARY."** Billy's rule, Bajonka's flyer, the prison gig.
- **"Is that a dog?"** A gag until it is the plan.
- **The clipboard.** Slapped away (Ch 2), signed blind (Ch 3), weaponized (Ch 5), read (Ch 7). Lucky's growth is literally paperwork.
- **SFX as objects.** CRASH tips dumpsters; KRAAANG knocks over a coffee; DOOM has gravity; Terms & Conditions are bricks; tweets are birds.
- **13/8.** A defect, a win, a loaf of challah, an assassination, an earthquake.
- **The ride cymbal.** Tinny in Ch 1, left behind, bought new in Ch 10, heard in Ch 12. Count-ins start every battle; the count-off is how you forgive.
- **Color is the HUD.** Lucky's pink/magenta/pale/purple; beige Dad; the amber edge; Phoenix's gray → cyan → gray by choice.
- **Potatoes.** Payment, merch, telepathic trauma, an investor pitch, Little Guy. **Cops in bad disguises:** mohawk, khakis, Kevin, a mop.
- **Themes:** creative labor under a fascist corporate state; noise vs. structure; home as something you rent vs. something you play; leaving and coming back; the pocket as trust; the empire gets a migraine and you're still broke.
