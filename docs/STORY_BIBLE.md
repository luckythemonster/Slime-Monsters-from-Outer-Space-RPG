## How to read this

- Canon is `docs/comic-outlines/` (Episode 0, Issues 1–6, Planets Collide, the Grand Finale). Every quoted line below is canon unless the beat is marked *(new)*; new lines exist only to carry Lucky's tweaks or to bridge chapters.
- This bible applies Lucky's seven plot tweaks (Lucky → Phoenix → Brian second → Brian gets Ryan in the room; Brian quits and Diego replaces him; Ryan becomes The Eternity, levels Palmer's, quits terrified, later says "because they suck"; Warner fills in, then Sticky; Billy Ruben's Han Solo moment; Planets Collide as the midpoint; the ending's direction with variants) and is the authority on story. Chapter 1 is the vertical slice.
- `docs/DESIGN.md` owns mechanics (battle rules, meters, scene ids). Mechanics named here are story requirements, not specs; where the two disagree on a beat, it is listed under Open questions.

## Voice rules

- Phoenix prefixes every thought with "My brain thinks…" and means it literally. The one time they stop (Cyan) is the scariest thing in the game; the first time they aim it at someone else (Dad, Ch 9) is the bravest.
- Ryan's captions are rigid square boxes, always the calmest text in the loudest room. He speaks aloud about once a chapter. "Yep." is a complete sentence.
- Diego says "man," reads auras, holds space, and is never wrong about vibes, only about everything else.
- Bajonka never speaks, never emotes beyond a sneeze, and is never explained. "Don't ask questions."
- Lucky's color is the HUD. The text never says what Lucky feels when the sprite can. Magenta makes them hit harder and worse.
- Villains speak corporate; the band speaks gig. Nobody says "evil." The Vanguard says "demographic," "synergy," "deliverables," "retention."
- Sound effects are objects. If a line has SFX, something in the room can be hit by it.
- Every chapter ends broke. If a chapter ends with money, it is a countdown.
- Jokes are performed, not explained. Shush/Woo. Ope. Yep.
- Brian is never a villain in his own mind.
- Toon Level is the presentation dial: 0 static, 1 shake, 2 SFX letters become physical objects, 3 the UI frame tears, 4 the art style ruptures. The story marks it; DESIGN.md implements it.

## Chapter outline

### Chapter 1 — Coagulation (VERTICAL SLICE)

**Locations:** Vanguard star-cruiser drop-pod bay (Planet California orbit) · Freefall over Minneapolis · the alley behind a dive bar · Cedar Avenue (hub street; Hard Luck Café; pawnshop; light rail) · Phoenix's apartment · the rooftops and a Northern Minnesota garage (cutscene) · City Sound Rehearsal Studio (sticker hallway, Room 4) · Palmer's Bar (exterior, interior, rubble). ~2 h.
**Party:** Lucky → +Phoenix → +Brian (second to join) → +Ryan → −Brian → −Ryan. Bajonka attaches at City Sound, field only, never controllable.

**Beats**

1. *Pod bay (Toon 1).* Lucky is a compressed, rebellious ball of pink-and-green slime stuffed into an escape pod. Dad's intercom balloons crowd the screen, exhausting parental complaint mixed with corporate-speak: "I just don't understand the whole nonbinary thing. Why can't you pick a tangible demographic to market yourself to? If you'd just join The Vanguard, you could be financially successful and oppress people with a dental plan!" The speaker is the only thing in the bay that matters; the rest is for jokes. Floor triggers keep him going *(new)*: "Your mother and I paid for the compression. The least you could do is market yourself." / "There's an opening in Demographic Alignment. You'd report to me." / "Billy's parents say Billy 'found themself.' In a storage unit. NO DENTAL. What kind of a brand is that?" / "…I can see the speaker light." (The Billy line is the first seed of Chapter 9.)
2. *The slap (Toon 3).* Tutorial verb: hold-and-release a pseudopod that **slaps the speaker mid-balloon** — SQUELCH — cutting Dad off: "mmf. MMMF. mmf—" Lucky: "Better." *(new)* This is the Chapter 9 boss mechanic, taught in the first minute. EJECT is locked while Dad is talking ("You can't eject while I'm talking, Lucky. Safety is a core value." *(new)*); once he is slimed, the unnecessarily red button works.
3. *Freefall (Toon 3).* The pod fires and breaks apart on hitting the atmosphere. Lucky flattens like a cartoon flying squirrel; spiky slime-sunglasses pop on. Steer the screaming pink-and-green contrail down through the purple-orange twilight and the WORK IS LOVE billboards; crossing one glitches it (Obnoxiousness, a hidden score). No fail state. The alley rushes up. Cut to black.
4. *The alley (Toon 2).* Snowy, slushy, behind a Minneapolis dive bar. Phoenix, a full humanoid slime in a ragged hoodie, holds a bag of trash and looks profoundly burnt out. KRATHOOM — Lucky craters the pavement feet from them; a giant physical "CRASH" sound effect manifests and knocks over the dumpsters one by one. Phoenix doesn't flinch; the trash bag hasn't moved. They push up their glasses: "My brain thinks you're going to have to pay the city for that pothole." Key item: **POTHOLE INVOICE — $1,400.** Never payable, never leaves the inventory, and it is the last object in the game.
5. *Coagulation.* Lucky peels off the pavement and snaps humanoid — pop-pop-pop. The rant: the fascist dad, the Bad Guys, the soul-crushing Planet California — and one new line: "My old band's still back there. Or they're not. I didn't check." Phoenix relates, pointing at the alley: came to Minnesota for college, the economy collapsed, rent is too high, permanently trapped in the Midwest. They look at each other and see it: the same underlying cosmic slime. A faint shared aura links them. Lucky: "I want to make the loudest, most obnoxious noise possible until this whole empire gets a migraine." Phoenix sighs at the trash bag, then at Lucky: "Beats the day job."
6. *Tutorial battle: THE RETRIEVAL DRONE.* A Vanguard drone that followed the pod whines in over the rooftops: "ASSET LOCATED. INITIATING RETRIEVAL. PLEASE HOLD FOR A REPRESENTATIVE." *(new)* Teaches: attacks are riffs; Lucky's sprite drops from pastel to magenta as HP falls; RIFF unlocks on round 2; Phoenix walks into frame and swings the trash bag. At 30% the drone says "RETRIEVAL ABORTED. BILLING ASSET." and dies on the next hit. Phoenix joins (and donates the band's whole treasury: four dollars). On a milk crate by the back door, a Shiba-mix with a clipboard watches. Phoenix: "That dog's been by the back door since I started here. My brain thinks she's the landlord." *(new)*
7. *Apartment (Toon 2).* Cramped, overpriced, band posters and unpaid bills. Lucky holds an unplugged, beat-up bass amp. Phoenix: "We need a drummer. I could put a flyer up at the coffee shop..." Lucky: "Too slow. I'm going to ask the universe." Hold to pour raw slime into the input jack; the arm glows violently; at full charge a neon soundwave erupts from the cone so hard it bends the dialogue frame outward.
8. *The signal (montage).* The wave rolls over the rooftops; a PRODUCTIVITY IS FREEDOM billboard glitches; it travels fast over highways and suburbs, north into the snowy pines. A detached garage in the dark. Inside: Ryan, stoic, heavy flannel, behind a massive kit. The wave washes completely over him. He freezes. Sticks hover. Eyes wide. He feels the rhythm of the cosmos. Caption: "End Part 1." He does not move. (This is the gag Lucky asked to keep: the universe calls Ryan; it just doesn't tell him where to go.)
9. *ONE WEEK LATER.* Nobody came. Phoenix: "The universe is a flyer at the coffee shop. I made one. Hard Luck Café. Corkboard. Go." *(new)* The street hub opens; enemies spawn (drones, a Vanguard intern, a feral scooter); the billboard glitches when Lucky walks under it.
10. *Hard Luck Café (Toon 0).* A corkboard with nine flyers for the same ska band. Choose the flyer text (DRUMMER WANTED — MUST PLAY FAST / NO COPS / SEEKING RHYTHMIC VESSEL FOR COSMIC PURPOSE). Leaving the café, the flyer delivers — not a drummer. **Brian.** Frank Zappa shirt, pristine blue jeans, a pedalboard the size of a door, radiating straight-laced suburbanite with too much disposable income. "I play guitar. I've got a $2,000 pedalboard and a completely normal amount of free time." Phoenix: "The flyer says drummer." Brian: "I read it as 'band.'" Lucky: "…Can you play loud?" Brian: "I can play *accurately*." *(new)* **Brian joins — second.** Commands: Normal Chord (reliable, tiny, feeds no meter) and Turn the Bass Down (debuffs Phoenix).
11. *Brian knows a guy.* "You need a drummer. I know a guy. My cousin's roommate up north. Doesn't talk. Never misses." *(new)* Cutscene: the garage, Ryan exactly as the wave left him a week ago, sticks still hovering. The wall landline rings twice. He unfreezes, crosses the garage, picks up. Brian: "Ryan. It's Brian. From the Zappa cover band. I've got a thing." Ryan: "Yep." Click. *(new)* Back on the street — Lucky: "The universe came through." Brian: "I called him." Lucky: "The UNIVERSE." Phoenix: "My brain thinks you're both right and that it doesn't matter." *(new)* The cosmic signal found Ryan; the aggressively normal guy got him in the room. Lucky never accepts the second half, and that denial is the engine of Chapter 10.
12. *City Sound hallway (Toon 0→1).* Twenty minutes of light rail. A dingy hallway layered in stickers and torn flyers. Ryan arrives carrying a physically impossible amount of drum hardware with Northern Minnesotan efficiency. Phoenix: "My brain thinks that's structurally impossible." Brian: "That's Ryan." *(new)* Outside Room 4, perfectly still, sits Bajonka, clipboard and pen by her paws. Ryan gives a single polite nod. Bajonka sneezes. Time-skip: Ryan is walking through the open door; Bajonka hasn't moved; the clipboard is gone. Phoenix: "My brain has questions. My brain is choosing not to ask them." *(new)* Bajonka's pose is the save point for the rest of the game.
13. *Room 4 (Toon 1).* Cramped and hot. Relaxed, Lucky and Phoenix are less humanoid and more melting puddles of neon goo holding instruments. Ryan sets up methodically. He does not stare at the alien slime monsters. He does not ask questions. He tightens a cymbal stand. Lucky: "You made good time from the transmission. You cool with playing fast?" Ryan: "Yep." Brian, fast: "I called him." Lucky's sprite does not turn. Phoenix, whispering loudly at the kit: "My brain thinks we should have asked his rate first. We have exactly four dollars and a half-ounce of weed." Ryan sits, clicks his sticks together twice.
14. *Mid-boss: THE AUDITION (Toon 3).* Lucky's foot stomps the distortion pedal; slime flares. "One, two, three, four!" Extreme close-up on the first snare hit — and Lucky's latent cosmic nature erupts: a glowing wave of green and pink blasts out of the amp and splatters entirely over Ryan and his kit; the screen jolts sideways and the gutter tears. The band is instantly at a blistering punk tempo. Enemies: WHRNNNG and THUD-THUD, giant jagged letters walling off the back of the room. Ryan is an uncontrollable ally: the slime fuses to his arms and chest and he plays inhumanly fast fills, a Looney-Tunes octopus smear, and every turn he either lands a huge Fill or Splatters the whole party. Beside the chaos, perfectly neat square captions are the clock: [Well. I guess my arms belong to the goo now.] … [Tempo's good, though. Very steady.] … [Ride cymbal sounds a bit tinny. Should probably buy a new one if I survive this.] Lucky screams in the zone; Phoenix plays bass looking terrified and deeply impressed. Reward: the Tinny Ride Cymbal.
15. *"You're hired."* The song crashes to a halt. Slime drips off the ceiling and the cymbals and Ryan, who is panting, encased in a thin layer of pulsing goo, sticks fused to his hands. Lucky: "Holy shit. You're a machine." [I am literally a hostage.] Ryan, deadpan: "So, do I get the gig?" Bajonka trots up from behind the kit, drops a fully drafted, multi-page, legally binding band agreement onto the snare, turns around, and sits down. Ryan joins; Bajonka attaches. Save: INITIAL HERE TO ACKNOWLEDGE RHYTHM SECTION.
16. *ONE MONTH LATER (duct-taped card, Toon 0).* Same room, re-dressed: the pedalboard and a boutique iced coffee in, the contract out. Brian points a pick at Lucky's stickered amp: "Do we really need all the political stickers? We're going to alienate the market." To Phoenix: "Hey, man, can you turn the bass down?" Phoenix, slime drooping: "I'm not a 'man,' Brian. And no." Ryan, tapping steady 4/4 on the hi-hat: [I give this guy three weeks. Tops.]
17. *Brian hates magic (Toon 2).* Mid-song, Lucky and Phoenix vibe, slime linking; Brian stands perfectly still playing soulless, sterile rock chords. The chorus hits; the slime syncs; the first full meter summons a massive physical "KRAAAAANG" in thick neon letters. The floating G swings around like a baseball bat and knocks over Brian's very expensive iced coffee. It spills completely over the pristine $2,000 pedalboard. Sparks. The music grinds to a halt. Brian, hands up: "I can't work like this! I hate this weird, creative shit! Can't we just play normal music?!" Key item: FRIED PEDALBOARD.
18. *ONE MONTH BEFORE THE POTATO BELT TOUR.* Phoenix: "My brain thinks that caption knows something we don't." *(new)* The room is cramped, hot, tense. Brian paces, jagged balloons taking up too much space: he's "not here to have fun," "not into this creative shit," the band is "wasting his time." Lucky reaches down and unplugs their guitar — POP. The slime on their shoulders bristles like an angry cat. Phoenix silences the bass with a glowing hand and takes half a step back, anticipating a nuclear explosion.
19. *The ultimatum (playable).* Lucky steps up to Brian, shockingly calm, which is scarier than yelling. "Listen to me very carefully, Brian." Three choices, all canon: "You need to stop being an asshole, or you are fired." / "We clearly have some fundamental differences in how we think a band should work, but that's something we can discuss." / "What I can't do is work with an asshole, so whatever's going on at home or wherever, don't bring it to rehearsal." Pick any order; Lucky says all three. Then: an X-ray cross-section of Brian's intensely fragile human brain. The rusty gears grind to a halt, spark, and spin backward.
20. *The misinterpretation (Toon 0→1).* Beet red, veins popping, a shaking finger: "Did you just say my foundation is broken?! Are you calling my house crooked?!" Dead silence. The background slime stops dripping mid-air. Phoenix: "My brain thinks you might be having a stroke." Lucky: "I literally didn't say anything about a house..." Brian, stuffing cables into his bag: "I know what you meant! My property values are fine! You're just jealous of my equity!" He storms out and slams the soundproof door so hard the hinges rattle. The silence is golden. [Well. His timing was terrible anyway.] **Brian leaves.**
21. *The flyer.* The door creaks. Bajonka trots in, walks to Lucky's mic stand, drops a crumpled flyer, sneezes, and leaves. "GIG TONIGHT: PALMER'S BAR - NO GUITAR SOLO NECESSARY." Lucky, quietly, to no one: "...Billy's rule." Phoenix: "Who?" Lucky: "Load the van." *(new)* Phoenix: "My brain thinks she's making a point." Palmer's is where Phoenix works. "My brain thinks this is fine." *(new)*
22. *Palmer's, pre-show (Toon 1).* Night on Cedar; cop duos patrol; a very tall stranger stands outside the bar and says the ceiling inside "has a heavy aura, man" (Diego, seeded). Inside: dark, crowded, a stage barely six inches off the floor — the tallest thing Lucky has ever stood on. Ryan behind the kit is already a menacing, bubbling mass of green slime, sticks at attention. In the crowd: Warner, dry-heaving after fronting Rat Fight as the opener (seeded); and one guy in a pristine leather jacket with the price tag on and a perfectly glued-on mohawk, tapping his collar: "Dispatch, I have located the unauthorized slime gathering. Stand by." Bajonka on a stool behind the bar is the last save.
23. *The first chord.* Lucky looks back at Ryan, over to Phoenix. They nod. No Brian to ruin the mix. Lucky and Phoenix hit the first massive power chord together; a visible shockwave of neon sound blows the front row's hair back and, at the back of the room, blows the cop's mohawk clean off his head into the tall stranger's beer. "whoa, man." *(new)* Ryan's arms blur: [No obnoxious guitar solos. Just pure, unadulterated rhythm. The slime is pulsating in 7/8 time. I can work with this.]
24. *BOSS: THE SET AT PALMER'S (Toon 3→4).* See Boss.
25. *The Eternity (Toon 4, scripted).* The gutters fill with neon static. Close on Ryan's slime-encased face: [I've been fighting the possession. That was a mistake.] He drops his shoulders and lets the cosmic rhythm take the wheel; green darkens to a deep bruised purple. [I am not trapped in the goo. The goo is trapped in the pocket.] The art ruptures — a cosmic-horror painting over a punk show. Ryan dissolves into pure swirling matter, a purplish-black localized black hole of rhythm. Giant gravity-heavy letters — DOOM, CRACK, THUD — orbit him. [Rhythm achieved.] One swings up and smashes through the drywall; the roof of Palmer's peels back exactly like a sardine can; the brick walls blow outward in slow motion; the crowd scatters into the night; the cop sprints off yelling into his collar, holding his mohawk. A final cymbal crash shakes the screen. The player cannot lose this fight and could not have stopped the building coming down — which is exactly what Chapter 6 inverts.
26. *Rubble (Toon 1).* Night sky where the ceiling was. Lucky and Phoenix untouched under plaster dust; Ryan solidifying back into a slime-covered human, holding two perfectly intact sticks. Lucky: "Okay. That is our sound." Phoenix: "My brain thinks we are officially domestic terrorists." Ryan, aloud for the first time all gig: "I think I broke the space-time continuum on that snare fill."
27. *The quit (new, Tweak 3).* Ryan looks down at his hands. They are still vibrating. He looks up at the sky where the ceiling used to be. [I could have killed every person in this room.] [That wasn't the goo.] [That was me.] Aloud: "I quit." Lucky goes magenta in a single frame. "We just found it. We just FOUND it—" Choice: RAGE ("You don't get to be scared of the best thing you've ever done!") / PLEAD ("One more show. One. You don't even have to let go.") / JOKE ("We'll put you on the flyer as a weather event."). Every branch is magenta, every branch is wrong, and every branch ends the same way: "Yep." He picks up nothing but his sticks and walks north. Phoenix: "My brain thinks he's scared." Lucky, locked magenta: "I don't care what he is." **Ryan leaves.** The Tinny Ride Cymbal stays in the inventory all game.
28. *Stinger.* From under a sheet of fallen drywall, Bajonka trots out, unfazed, the van keys in her mouth and a folded itinerary under her collar with "POTATO BELT: TOMORROW" circled in red marker. Lucky: "We don't have a drummer." Bajonka sneezes. In the rubble behind them, two silhouettes who didn't flee: the tall stranger holding a dripping mohawk, and Warner holding his sticks. NEXT: THE POTATO BELT. (DRUMMER WANTED.) Post-credit *(new)*: on Planet California, a beige hand stamps a memo: ASSET LOCATED. BILL TO RETENTION.

**Boss — THE SET AT PALMER'S (Structural Integrity 100%).** The venue is the enemy and you want to kill it. Targets: the Undercover Cop, whose DISPATCH bar ticks each round he survives (full = raid, retry), plus a CROWD HOSTILITY "mood" enemy that doesn't attack. Riffs damage STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY. The shared POCKET gauge fills with every party action, faster when Lucky and Phoenix act back-to-back; Ryan plays every turn, uncontrolled. At 50%: [The slime is pulsating in 7/8 time.] At 75%: [I've been fighting the possession. That was a mistake.] — and the only choice Ryan gets all game, **[Keep fighting it] / [Let go]**. Fighting it ticks Dispatch; Let go triggers Toon 4 and beat 25. The gauge fills regardless of KOs, so the set cannot be lost; a full-party KO just makes the rest of it a drum solo. The player chose "let go," and Ryan pays for it: that is why the quitting stings.

**Stinger:** beat 28 — no drummer, no guitarist, a stolen van, a tour tomorrow.

## Midpoint design

Planets Collide is the hinge because it is the only fight the band's noise cannot win, and it takes away everything Disc 1 accumulated at once: the apartment Phoenix sold a month of silence for, the van, the coupon, Shannon's armchair, the planet, and the laser guitar Lucky's whole identity has run through since the pod bay.

Disc 1 teaches the same rule five times: the band's noise breaks rooms. Palmer's roof, the silo, the dome, the judges' table, the Panopticon's silence. Chapter 6 offers a sixth room — the sky — and the player, holding every tool the game has given them, cannot move the bar. The two bosses that frame Disc 1 are the same fight from opposite sides: Chapter 1's Palmer's set *cannot be lost* and the player could not avoid levelling the building (the only input was LET GO); Chapter 6's Sludge Duel *cannot be won* and every input works while nothing matters. INTERMISSION sits between them. It is the game's one honest lie: the HUD never fakes a number, but the bar is labeled GRAVITY and the game-over card is a fake "turn the cartridge over."

What flips at the midpoint: **home** (eviction threats → no planet); **the instrument** (RIFF locked by contract in Ch 5 → gone in Ch 7, answered by Lucky's voice); **Phoenix** (bass → synth; puddle → choosing when to go Cyan); **the Vanguard** (khakis at a mosh pit → the band is wanted); **the rhythm section** (Ryan ran → Ryan comes back; Sticky's 13/8 goes from defect to the only thing that breaks a paywall); **Lucky** (reactive magenta → leading, in magenta, by choice; the last line of Disc 1 is the first time Lucky leads instead of rages).

Balancing the halves: the comic is front-loaded, so Disc 1 compresses nine scripts into six chapters (Issues 2 and 3 are one night; Issues 5 and 6 are one embargo), each turning on a relationship (Phoenix; Brian/Ryan; Diego/Warner; Alibi; the landlord; Kurt). Disc 2 builds six chapters from the Grand Finale's five parts, each fused to a thread the comic only seeded: the Luthier run gets Phoenix's song and the Eclipse; Kurt gets grief and a job; Planet California gets Dad, Billy and the ship from screen one; Minnesota gets the lie; the prison gets Pete; the yacht gets everyone. Disc 1 is losing people. Disc 2 is going back for them. Both discs run about nine hours.

## Billy Ruben

**Who.** A bilirubin-amber slime — jaundice yellow, sun-bleached at the edges; the joke is in the name — bassist and wall-of-sound engineer, now pirate DJ of Radio Free California inside a hollowed-out WORK IS LOVE billboard. Pronouns they/them (Dad's "Billy 'found themself'" is the tell). Billy holds walls of sound; Lucky writes the songs that stand on them. Billy is the author of the only rule their old band ever had — **NO GUITAR SOLO NECESSARY** — which is why that phrase on Bajonka's flyer in Chapter 1 stops Lucky cold, and why it comes back on the prison flyer in Chapter 10.

**Backstory.** Lucky and Billy were **NO DENTAL**, two teenage slimes in a Planet California storage unit playing noise under a WORK IS LOVE billboard — the band named for the one thing Dad kept offering. Dad shut the unit down as unmarketable and had the biggest show of their lives raided; Lucky was dragged onto the star-cruiser, and the pod bay on screen one is where that night ends. There were two pods. Lucky slapped the intercom and hit EJECT without checking the next one. Billy walked into the bay as the pod fired and stood under the contrail through the glass. Lucky has spent the whole story telling themself Billy didn't show ("Or they're not. I didn't check."). Billy didn't chase. Billy kept the amp, recorded a tape, built a barge out of a billboard, and when Lucky "asked the universe" in Chapter 1 the wave reached Planet California; Billy has followed its echoes across the galaxy, one gig behind, ever since. The trail was never hard: a contrail over Minneapolis, a pothole, a levelled bar, a trench of potato slime, a shattered dome, a planet that isn't there.

**The Han Solo moment (Chapter 9, beat 10).** It is the literal Death Star: the ship Lucky escaped from, come back for them. Dad's star-cruiser descends over the Lifestyle Tower roof, the tractor beam locks on Lucky, the planet's power is cut, the amps die, and the party's commands grey out one by one. "My brain thinks this is the part where we get re-podded." Then every billboard on the planet plays the NO DENTAL riff at once; a converted billboard-barge drops out of hyperspace, refrigerator coils glowing, and rakes the beam emitter with a wall of sound. The beam stutters; the hubcap guitar draws power from the broadcast; the commands light back up; *the player* takes the shot. "You left the amp on, dude. I could hear you from here." Billy drops into the fifth party slot as a guest. Command: **WALL OF SOUND** — a sustain that holds a battle's state open (taunts everything, prevents phase advance) for a round; mechanically it is what lets Lucky put the guitar down in Chapter 12.

**Why there.** (1) Chapter 9 asks whether Lucky's old life was real or just something they fled, and Billy is the answer: a bandmate who stayed and never held the leaving against them. (2) That forgiveness is the hinge for Chapter 10: Billy forgives Lucky for leaving so Lucky can forgive Ryan for leaving; Lucky's rage at Ryan was always partly guilt about Billy. (3) Han clears the shot; he does not take it. The beam is blown by Lucky's first full chord on the hubcap guitar, so the rescue rewards growth instead of replacing it. (4) At the final boss the arrival would compete with the fourth-wall break for the spotlight; here it is the climax of its own chapter, and in Chapter 12 Billy is already part of the band that Trunk is lying about. (5) The rhythm-section wound (Ryan) and the old-band wound (Billy) are answered in consecutive chapters by two different kinds of coming back: one person came for Lucky; Lucky went for the other.

## Ending variants

All three keep the direction Lucky likes — Laserstein's staged suicide, Darnell Trunk, the paywall, the golden toilet, the tweet-birds, the fourth-wall break, still broke because of Artisanal Anti-Matter — and all three end on the elephant head bungeed to the hood, the gold cockpit, and "My brain thinks... my brain thinks it's a hundred thousand credits per gallon." / "We better sell some merch at this next one, man."

**V1 — Roll Him Up (closest to the comic; recommended, with V3's banked damage underneath).** Lucky's critique physically breaks the game. Trunk's 16-color sprite shatters like glass; behind it, on a flat white field, is a deliberately *wrong* asset — a hyper-realistic, Dadaist photo-collage at the wrong resolution, JPEG-artifacted, breaking the game's palette rule. The command menu corrupts into a gray OS dialog with one button. *Mechanics:* every normal command is gone; the one input is **ROLL** — Lucky's cartoon hand becomes the cursor and the player drags it into the collage to roll the cutouts into a massive paper joint; Billy's Wall of Sound holds the battle open until the drag completes. Lucky lights it off the plasma-jet strings, takes a drag, and exhales a cloud of pink smoke. The boss is defeated. *Last beat:* the cockpit, the fuel gauge, Diego unplugging the talk radio, Lucky plugging in, coordinates for the next gig to pay for gas.

**V2 — Allegory: OFF.** Lucky's complaint opens the game's own Options menu over the fight. A new toggle sits at the bottom — ALLEGORY [ON] — in a row that has been empty on every options screen all game. OFF strips Trunk to a magenta-and-black checkerboard placeholder labeled RICH GUY (TEMP). Lucky rolls the placeholder and smokes it. *Mechanics:* Phase 3 is a menu puzzle: the player has to leave the battle UI, find the toggle, and flip it while the birds keep screaming; nothing in the battle can hurt them during the menu, which is the point. *Last beat:* the checkerboard cube bungeed to the hood; Artisanal Anti-Matter; Diego: "Honestly the placeholder had a better aura."

**V3 — The Scene.** Lucky turns from the camera to the crowd — and Billy's barge is a clown car: the cyborg farmers, the Terrifying Librarian (one SHHHH deletes every bird on screen), Greg with two waters, Kevin, the Tall Guitarist's finger, Warner, Shannon, Little Guy. The final command is **EVERYONE**; a hundred hands roll the elephant, and the smoke spells NOISE IS LOVE where WORK IS LOVE was. *Mechanics:* every chapter's CROWD meter has been secretly banked since Palmer's; Phase 3 damage is that total, so the ending scales with the whole tour. *Last beat:* the cockpit, the fuel gauge; Phoenix: "My brain thinks we can charge them for the ride." Little Guy, from the cup holder, finally secure: "I am a complex starch."

## Running gags, motifs, themes

- **Bajonka handled it.** Clipboard → contract → flyer → van keys → the dock guard → the Denim Boys → claymore → dossier → escape pod → prison break → the yacht's title paperwork. Each stinger she appears in is the next chapter's premise. Her one blind spot is Pete.
- **Every chapter ends broke.** The pothole bill, four dollars, the eviction, the deposit, the fuel gauge. The economy is the antagonist; Trunk is its face.
- **The Pothole Invoice** rides in the inventory to the credits, accruing fees.
- **"NO GUITAR SOLO NECESSARY."** Billy's rule, Bajonka's flyer, the prison gig. Ryan's first caption at Palmer's is the same idea in his words.
- **"Is that a dog?"** A gag until it is the plan.
- **The clipboard.** Slapped away (Ch 2), signed blind (Ch 3), signed for Phoenix (Ch 4), weaponized (Ch 5), read (Ch 7). Lucky's growth is literally paperwork. Clipboards on both sides: the Eclipse and the Vanguard are one bureaucracy with different stickers.
- **SFX as objects.** CRASH tips dumpsters; KRAAANG knocks over a coffee; DOOM has gravity; Terms & Conditions are bricks; tweets are birds.
- **13/8.** A defect, a win, a loaf of challah, an assassination, an earthquake.
- **The cosmic signal vs. the phone call.** Ryan (frozen), Warner (three weeks late), Billy (a galaxy away) — versus Brian's landline. Lucky never credits the landline.
- **The ride cymbal.** Tinny in Ch 1, left behind, bought new in Ch 10, heard in Ch 12.
- **Count-ins start every battle.** Sticky's is WHIRRR-CLANK-SQUEEEAAAK. The count-off is how you forgive.
- **Color is the HUD.** Lucky's pink/magenta/pale/purple; beige Dad; the amber edge; Phoenix's gray → cyan → gray by choice.
- **"My brain thinks."** Lost under Cyan, recovered as the sound of the spell breaking, finally aimed at Dad.
- **Cops in bad disguises, escalating:** mohawk, khakis, Intern Unit 74-B, a mop.
- **Potatoes.** Payment, merch, telepathic trauma, an investor pitch, a truffle who demands an ambassador, Little Guy.
- **Countdowns that are never money:** eviction, DAY 14/30, 00:00:01, PLANET PROXIMITY, the fuel gauge.
- **WORK IS LOVE** billboards glitch when the band plays; the last one says NOISE IS LOVE (V3) or just glitches (V1/V2).
- **Letting go vs. holding on.** Ryan lets go and runs; Lucky holds on and rages; Phoenix lets go of "my brain thinks" and becomes something scarier; Kurt holds a planet together; Lucky wins by putting the guitar down.
- **Themes:** creative labor under a fascist corporate state; noise vs. structure, and learning structure without losing the noise; home as something you rent vs. something you play; leaving and coming back; the pocket as trust; the empire gets a migraine and you're still broke.
