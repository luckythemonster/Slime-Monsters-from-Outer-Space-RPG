## Voice rules

- Phoenix prefixes every thought with "My brain thinks…" and means it literally. The one time they stop (Cyan) is the scariest thing in the game; the first time they aim it at someone else (Dad) is the bravest.
- Ryan's captions are rigid square boxes, the calmest text in the loudest room. He speaks aloud about once a chapter. "Yep." is a complete sentence.
- Diego says "man," reads auras, holds space, and is never wrong about vibes, only about everything else.
- Bajonka never speaks, never emotes beyond a sneeze, and is never explained. "Don't ask questions."
- Lucky's color is the HUD. The text never says what Lucky feels when the sprite can. Magenta hits harder and worse.
- Villains speak corporate; the band speaks gig. Nobody says "evil." The Vanguard says "demographic," "synergy," "deliverables," "retention."
- Sound effects are objects. If a line has SFX, something in the room can be hit by it.
- Every chapter ends broke. If a chapter ends with money, it is a countdown.
- Jokes are performed, not explained. Shush/Woo. Ope. Yep. Brian is never a villain in his own mind.
- Toon Level is the presentation dial: 0 static, 1 shake, 2 SFX letters become objects, 3 the UI frame tears, 4 the art style ruptures.

## Chapter outline

### Chapter 1 — Coagulation (VERTICAL SLICE)

**Locations:** Vanguard star-cruiser drop-pod bay (Planet California orbit) · Freefall over Minneapolis · the alley behind a dive bar · Cedar Avenue hub (Hard Luck Café, pawnshop, light rail) · Phoenix's apartment · rooftops and a Northern Minnesota garage (cutscene) · City Sound Rehearsal Studio (sticker hallway, Room 4) · Palmer's Bar (exterior, interior, rubble). ~2 h.
**Party:** Lucky → +Phoenix → +Brian (second to join) → +Ryan → −Brian → −Ryan. Bajonka attaches at City Sound, field only, never controllable.

**Beats**

1. *Pod bay (Toon 1).* Lucky is a compressed, rebellious ball of pink-and-green slime stuffed into an escape pod. Dad's intercom balloons crowd the screen: "I just don't understand the whole nonbinary thing. Why can't you pick a tangible demographic to market yourself to? If you'd just join The Vanguard, you could be financially successful and oppress people with a dental plan!" Floor triggers keep him going *(new)*: "Your mother and I paid for the compression. The least you could do is market yourself." / "There's an opening in Demographic Alignment. You'd report to me." / "Billy's parents say Billy 'found themself.' In a storage unit. NO DENTAL. What kind of a brand is that?" / "…I can see the speaker light." (The Billy line is the first seed of Chapter 9.)
2. *The slap (Toon 3).* Tutorial verb: hold-and-release a pseudopod that **slaps the speaker mid-balloon** — SQUELCH — "mmf. MMMF. mmf—" Lucky: "Better." *(new)* This is the Chapter 9 boss mechanic, taught in minute one. EJECT is locked while Dad is talking ("You can't eject while I'm talking, Lucky. Safety is a core value." *(new)*); slimed, the unnecessarily red button works.
3. *Freefall (Toon 3).* The pod breaks apart on the atmosphere. Lucky flattens like a cartoon flying squirrel; spiky slime-sunglasses pop on. Steer the screaming pink-and-green contrail down through the purple-orange twilight and the WORK IS LOVE billboards; crossing one glitches it (a hidden Obnoxiousness score). No fail state. The alley rushes up. Cut to black.
4. *The alley (Toon 2).* Snow and slush behind a Minneapolis dive bar. Phoenix, a humanoid slime in a ragged hoodie, holds a trash bag and looks profoundly burnt out. KRATHOOM — Lucky craters the pavement; a giant physical "CRASH" manifests and knocks over the dumpsters one by one. Phoenix doesn't flinch; the bag hasn't moved. They push up their glasses: "My brain thinks you're going to have to pay the city for that pothole." Key item: **POTHOLE INVOICE — $1,400.** Never payable, never leaves the inventory.
5. *Coagulation.* Lucky peels off the pavement and snaps humanoid — pop-pop-pop. The rant: the fascist dad, the Bad Guys, Planet California — and one new line: "My old band's still back there. Or they're not. I didn't check." Phoenix: came for college, the economy collapsed, rent is too high, trapped in the Midwest. They look at each other and see the same cosmic slime; a faint shared aura links them. Lucky: "I want to make the loudest, most obnoxious noise possible until this whole empire gets a migraine." Phoenix, to the trash bag, then to Lucky: "Beats the day job."
6. *Tutorial battle: THE RETRIEVAL DRONE.* A Vanguard drone that followed the pod: "ASSET LOCATED. INITIATING RETRIEVAL. PLEASE HOLD FOR A REPRESENTATIVE." *(new)* Teaches: attacks are riffs; Lucky's sprite drops from pastel to magenta as HP falls; RIFF unlocks round 2; Phoenix walks into frame and swings the trash bag. At 30%: "RETRIEVAL ABORTED. BILLING ASSET." Phoenix joins and donates the treasury: four dollars. On a milk crate by the back door, a Shiba-mix with a clipboard watches. Phoenix: "That dog's been by the back door since I started here. My brain thinks she's the landlord." *(new)*
7. *Apartment (Toon 2).* Band posters, unpaid bills, an unplugged beat-up bass amp. Phoenix: "We need a drummer. I could put a flyer up at the coffee shop..." Lucky: "Too slow. I'm going to ask the universe." Hold to pour raw slime into the input jack; the arm glows violently; at full charge a neon soundwave erupts from the cone so hard it bends the dialogue frame outward.
8. *The signal (montage).* The wave rolls over the rooftops; a PRODUCTIVITY IS FREEDOM billboard glitches; it heads north over highways into the snowy pines. A detached garage in the dark. Ryan, stoic, heavy flannel, behind a massive kit. The wave washes completely over him. He freezes. Sticks hover. He feels the rhythm of the cosmos. Caption: "End Part 1." He does not move. 9. *ONE WEEK LATER.* Nobody came. Phoenix: "The universe is a flyer at the coffee shop. I made one. Hard Luck Café. Corkboard. Go." *(new)* The hub opens; enemies spawn (drones, a Vanguard intern, a feral scooter).

Brian: "I can play *accurately*." *(new)* **Brian joins — second** (Tweak 1). Commands: Normal Chord (reliable, tiny, feeds no meter) and Turn the Bass Down (debuffs Phoenix).
11. *Brian knows a guy.* "You need a drummer. I know a guy. My cousin's roommate up north. Doesn't talk. **Boss — THE SET AT PALMER'S (Structural Integrity 100%).** The venue is the enemy and you want to kill it. Targets: the Undercover Cop, whose DISPATCH bar ticks each round he survives (full = raid, retry), plus a CROWD HOSTILITY "mood" enemy that never attacks. Riffs damage STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY. The shared POCKET gauge fills with every party action, faster when Lucky and Phoenix act back-to-back; Ryan plays every turn, uncontrolled. At 50%: [The slime is pulsating in 7/8 time.] At 75%: [I've been fighting the possession. That was a mistake.] — and the only choice Ryan gets all game, **[Keep fighting it] / [Let go]**. Fighting it ticks Dispatch; Let go triggers Toon 4 and beat 25. The gauge fills regardless of KOs, so the set cannot be lost; a full-party KO makes the rest a drum solo. The player chose "let go," and Ryan pays for it: that is why the quitting stings.

**Stinger:** beat 28 — no drummer, no guitarist, a stolen van, a tour tomorrow.

## Midpoint design

Planets Collide is the hinge because it is the only fight the band's noise cannot win, and it takes away everything Disc 1 accumulated at once: the apartment Phoenix sold a month of silence for, the van, the coupon, the planet, and the laser guitar Lucky's whole identity has run through since the pod bay.

Disc 1 teaches one rule five times: the band's noise breaks rooms — Palmer's roof, the silo, the dome, the judges' table, the Panopticon's silence. Chapter 6 offers a sixth room, the sky, and the player, holding every tool the game has given them, cannot move the bar. The two bosses that frame Disc 1 are the same fight from opposite sides: the Palmer's set *cannot be lost* and the player could not avoid levelling the building (the only input was LET GO); the Sludge Duel *cannot be won* and every input works while nothing matters. INTERMISSION sits between them. It is the game's one honest lie: the HUD never fakes a number, but the bar is labeled GRAVITY and the game-over card is a fake "turn the cartridge over."

What flips: **home** (eviction threats → no planet); **the instrument** (RIFF locked by contract in Ch 5 → gone in Ch 7, answered by Lucky's voice); **Phoenix** (bass → synth; puddle → choosing when to go Cyan); **the Vanguard** (khakis at a mosh pit → the band is wanted); **the rhythm section** (Ryan ran → Ryan comes back; 13/8 goes from defect to the only thing that breaks a paywall); **Lucky** (reactive magenta → leading, in magenta, by choice; the last line of Disc 1 is the first time Lucky leads instead of rages).

Balancing the halves: the comic is front-loaded, so Disc 1 compresses nine scripts into six chapters (Issues 2 and 3 are one night; 5 and 6 one embargo), each turning on a relationship (Phoenix; Brian/Ryan; Diego/Warner; Alibi; the landlord; Kurt). Disc 2 builds six chapters from the Grand Finale's five parts, each fused to a thread the comic only seeded: the Luthier run gets Phoenix's song and the Eclipse; Kurt gets grief and a job; Planet California gets Dad, Billy and the ship from screen one; Minnesota gets the lie; the prison gets Pete; the yacht gets everyone. Disc 1 is losing people. Disc 2 is going back for them. About nine hours each.

## Billy Ruben

**Who.** A bilirubin-amber slime — jaundice yellow, sun-bleached at the edges; the joke is in the name — bassist and wall-of-sound engineer, now pirate DJ of Radio Free California inside a hollowed-out WORK IS LOVE billboard. They/them (Dad's "Billy 'found themself'" is the tell). Billy holds walls of sound; Lucky writes the songs that stand on them. Billy wrote the only rule their old band ever had — **NO GUITAR SOLO NECESSARY** — which is why that phrase on Bajonka's flyer stops Lucky cold in Chapter 1, and why it returns on the prison flyer in Chapter 10.

**Backstory.** Lucky and Billy were **NO DENTAL**, two teenage slimes in a Planet California storage unit playing noise under a WORK IS LOVE billboard — the band named for the one thing Dad kept offering. Dad shut the unit down as unmarketable and had their biggest show raided; Lucky was dragged onto the star-cruiser, and the pod bay on screen one is where that night ends. There were two pods. Lucky slapped the intercom and hit EJECT without checking the next one. Billy walked into the bay as the pod fired and stood under the contrail through the glass. Lucky has spent the whole story telling themself Billy didn't show ("Or they're not. I didn't check."). Billy didn't chase. Billy kept the amp, recorded a tape, built a barge out of a billboard, and when Lucky "asked the universe" in Chapter 1 the wave reached Planet California; Billy has followed its echoes one gig behind ever since. The trail was never hard: a contrail, a pothole, a levelled bar, a trench of potato slime, a shattered dome, a planet that isn't there.

**The Han Solo moment (Chapter 9, beat 10).** It is the literal Death Star: the ship Lucky escaped from, come back for them. The tractor beam locks on, the power is cut, the amps die, the commands grey out. "My brain thinks this is the part where we get re-podded." Then every billboard on the planet plays the NO DENTAL riff at once; a billboard-barge drops out of hyperspace and rakes the beam emitter with a wall of sound; the beam stutters; the hubcap guitar draws power from the broadcast; *the player* takes the shot. "You left the amp on, dude. I could hear you from here." Billy drops into the fifth party slot as a guest. Command: **WALL OF SOUND** — a sustain that holds a battle's state open for a round (taunts everything, blocks phase advance); mechanically it is what lets Lucky put the guitar down in Chapter 12.

**Why there.** (1) Chapter 9 asks whether Lucky's old life was real or just something they fled, and Billy is the answer: a bandmate who stayed and never held the leaving against them. (2) That forgiveness is the hinge for Chapter 10: Billy forgives Lucky for leaving so Lucky can forgive Ryan for leaving; Lucky's rage at Ryan was always partly guilt about Billy. (3) Han clears the shot; he does not take it. The beam is blown by Lucky's own chord, so the rescue rewards growth instead of replacing it. (4) At the final boss the arrival would compete with the fourth-wall break for the spotlight; here it is the climax of its own chapter, and in Chapter 12 Billy is already part of the band Trunk is lying about. (5) The rhythm-section wound (Ryan) and the old-band wound (Billy) are answered in consecutive chapters by two kinds of coming back: one person came for Lucky; Lucky went for the other.

## Ending variants

All three keep the direction Lucky likes — Laserstein's staged suicide, Darnell Trunk, the paywall, the golden toilet, the tweet-birds, the fourth-wall break, still broke because of Artisanal Anti-Matter — and all end on the elephant head bungeed to the hood, the gold cockpit, and "My brain thinks... my brain thinks it's a hundred thousand credits per gallon." / "We better sell some merch at this next one, man."

**V1 — Roll Him Up (closest to the comic; recommended, with V3's banked damage underneath).** Lucky's critique physically breaks the game. Trunk's 16-color sprite shatters like glass; behind it, on a flat white field, is a deliberately *wrong* asset — a hyper-realistic, Dadaist photo-collage at the wrong resolution, JPEG-artifacted, breaking the game's palette rule. The command menu corrupts into a gray OS dialog with one button. *Mechanics:* every normal command is gone; the one input is **ROLL** — Lucky's cartoon hand becomes the cursor and the player drags it into the collage to roll the cutouts into a massive paper joint; Billy's Wall of Sound holds the battle open until the drag completes. Lucky lights it off the plasma-jet strings, takes a drag, and exhales pink smoke. *Last beat:* the cockpit, the fuel gauge, Diego unplugging the talk radio, Lucky plugging in, coordinates for the next gig to pay for gas.

**V2 — Allegory: OFF.** Lucky's complaint opens the game's own Options menu over the fight. A new toggle sits at the bottom — ALLEGORY [ON] — in a row that has been empty on every options screen all game. OFF strips Trunk to a magenta-and-black checkerboard placeholder labeled RICH GUY (TEMP). Lucky rolls the placeholder and smokes it. *Mechanics:* Phase 3 is a menu puzzle — leave the battle UI, find the toggle, flip it while the birds keep screaming; nothing in the battle can hurt the party during the menu, which is the point. *Last beat:* the checkerboard cube bungeed to the hood; Artisanal Anti-Matter; Diego: "Honestly the placeholder had a better aura."

**V3 — The Scene.** Lucky turns from the camera to the crowd — and Billy's barge is a clown car: the cyborg farmers, the Terrifying Librarian (one SHHHH deletes every bird on screen), Greg with two waters, Kevin, the Tall Guitarist's finger, Warner, Shannon, Little Guy. The final command is **EVERYONE**; a hundred hands roll the elephant, and the smoke spells NOISE IS LOVE where WORK IS LOVE was. *Mechanics:* every chapter's CROWD meter has been secretly banked since Palmer's; Phase 3 damage is that total, so the ending scales with the whole tour. *Last beat:* the cockpit, the fuel gauge; Phoenix: "My brain thinks we can charge them for the ride." Little Guy, from the cup holder, finally secure: "I am a complex starch."

## Running gags, motifs, themes

- **Bajonka handled it.** Clipboard → contract → flyer → van keys → the dock guard → the Denim Boys → claymore → dossier → escape pod → prison break → the yacht's title paperwork. Each stinger she appears in is the next chapter's premise. Her one blind spot is Pete.
- **Every chapter ends broke.** The pothole bill, four dollars, the eviction, the deposit, the fuel gauge. The economy is the antagonist; Trunk is its face. The **Pothole Invoice** rides in the inventory to the credits, accruing fees.
- **"NO GUITAR SOLO NECESSARY."** Billy's rule, Bajonka's flyer, the prison gig; Ryan's first caption at Palmer's is the same idea in his words.
- **"Is that a dog?"** A gag until it is the plan.
- **The clipboard.** Slapped away (Ch 2), signed blind (Ch 3), signed for Phoenix (Ch 4), weaponized (Ch 5), read (Ch 7). Lucky's growth is literally paperwork. The Eclipse and the Vanguard are one bureaucracy with different stickers.
- **SFX as objects.** CRASH tips dumpsters; KRAAANG knocks over a coffee; DOOM has gravity; Terms & Conditions are bricks; tweets are birds.
- **13/8.** A defect, a win, a loaf of challah, an assassination, an earthquake.
- **The signal vs. the phone call.** Ryan (frozen), Warner (three weeks late), Billy (a galaxy away) — versus Brian's landline. Lucky never credits the landline.
- **The ride cymbal.** Tinny in Ch 1, left behind, bought new in Ch 10, heard in Ch 12. **Count-ins** start every battle; Sticky's is WHIRRR-CLANK-SQUEEEAAAK; the count-off is how you forgive.
- **Color is the HUD.** Lucky's pink/magenta/pale/purple; beige Dad; the amber edge; Phoenix's gray → cyan → gray by choice. **"My brain thinks"** — lost under Cyan, recovered as the sound of the spell breaking, finally aimed at Dad.
- **Cops in bad disguises, escalating:** mohawk, khakis, Intern Unit 74-B, a mop.
- **Potatoes.** Payment, merch, telepathic trauma, an investor pitch, a truffle who demands an ambassador, Little Guy.
- **Countdowns that are never money:** eviction, DAY 14/30, 00:00:01, PLANET PROXIMITY, the fuel gauge.
- **WORK IS LOVE** billboards glitch when the band plays; the last one says NOISE IS LOVE (V3) or just glitches.
- **Letting go vs. holding on.** Ryan lets go and runs; Lucky holds on and rages; Phoenix lets go of "my brain thinks" and becomes something scarier; Kurt holds a planet together; Lucky wins by putting the guitar down.
- **Themes:** creative labor under a fascist corporate state; noise vs. structure, and learning structure without losing the noise; home as something you rent vs. something you play; leaving and coming back; the pocket as trust; the empire gets a migraine and you're still broke.
