# Slime Monsters from Outer Space — Story & Character Bible (JRPG Build)

Design stance: every chapter turns on a relationship. The plot is a tour; the story is who leaves, who stays, and who comes back.

## Chapter outline

### Chapter 1 — Coagulation (VERTICAL SLICE)

**Locations:** Vanguard star-cruiser pod bay; Minneapolis sky (free-fall); the alley behind the dive bar; Phoenix's apartment; Minneapolis streets and coffee shop; Ryan's garage (cutscene); City Sound Rehearsal Studio (hallway, Room 4); Palmer's Bar (exterior, interior, rubble).
**Party:** Lucky → +Phoenix → +Brian (temp) → +Ryan (temp) → −Brian → −Ryan. Bajonka attaches at City Sound.

**Beats**
1. *Pod bay (Toon 1).* Lucky is a compressed ball of slime in an escape pod. Dad on the intercom: "I just don't understand the whole nonbinary thing. Why can't you pick a tangible demographic to market yourself to? If you'd just join The Vanguard, you could be financially successful and oppress people with a dental plan!" The only interactable is the speaker.
2. *(Toon 3)* A pseudopod slaps the speaker — SQUELCH — cutting Dad mid-balloon. The unnecessarily red EJECT. The pod breaks up; Lucky flattens like a flying squirrel; slime sunglasses form. Steering minigame: paint the pink-and-green contrail over the WORK IS LOVE billboards. (The contrail is the trail Billy follows.)
3. *Alley (Toon 2).* Phoenix, hoodie, trash bag, burnt out. KRATHOOM — Lucky craters the pavement; a physical CRASH knocks over dumpsters. Phoenix pushes up their glasses. "My brain thinks you're going to have to pay the city for that pothole."
4. Lucky pop-pop-pops humanoid and rants: fascist dad, the Bad Guys, Planet California — plus one new line: "My old band's still back there. Or they're not. I didn't check." Phoenix: college, collapsed economy, rent, trapped in the Midwest. A shared aura links them. Lucky: "I want to make the loudest, most obnoxious noise possible until this whole empire gets a migraine." Phoenix: "Beats the day job."
5. *Tutorial battle.* A Vanguard Retrieval Drone that followed the pod. Teaches sets: attacks are riffs, Lucky's color shifts with damage, Phoenix's trash bag is a weapon. Phoenix joins.
6. *Apartment (Toon 2).* Posters, unpaid bills, an unplugged bass amp. Phoenix: "We need a drummer. I could put a flyer up at the coffee shop..." Lucky: "Too slow. I'm going to ask the universe." Slime into the input jack; the soundwave bends the screen border outward.
7. *Signal montage.* A PRODUCTIVITY IS FREEDOM billboard glitches; the wave rolls north into the pines. Ryan in his garage, flannel, sticks hovering. He feels the rhythm of the cosmos. He does not move. Caption: "End Part 1."
8. *One week later.* Nobody came. Phoenix put the flyer up anyway. The flyer worked: **Brian**, Zappa shirt, pristine jeans, enormous pedalboard. "Do we really need all the political stickers? We're going to alienate the market." "Hey, man, can you turn the bass down?" Phoenix: "I'm not a 'man,' Brian. And no." Brian joins: sterile chords, high accuracy, zero crit.
9. Brian: "You need a drummer. I know a guy. My cousin's roommate up north. Doesn't talk. Never misses." Cutscene: the call reaches the garage where Ryan has sat since the wave. "Ryan. It's Brian. From the Zappa cover band. I've got a thing." Ryan: "Yep." The cosmic signal found him; the aggressively normal guy got him in the room. Lucky will never believe the second half.
10. *City Sound (Toon 0–1).* Sticker hallway. Ryan carries a physically impossible amount of hardware. Outside Room 4: Bajonka, clipboard, pen. Ryan nods once. Bajonka sneezes. Time-skip: door open, clipboard gone.
11. Room 4. Lucky and Phoenix are melting puddles with instruments. Ryan tightens a cymbal stand and asks nothing. Lucky: "You made good time from the transmission. You cool with playing fast?" Ryan: "Yep." Brian: "I called him." Lucky does not hear this. Phoenix: "My brain thinks we should have asked his rate first. We have exactly four dollars and a half-ounce of weed."
12. *Mid-boss: AUDITION (Toon 3).* Pedal stomp. "One, two, three, four!" First snare hit: Lucky's cosmic nature erupts and splatters Ryan and the kit; the screen jolts and tears. Ryan is an ally whose slime-controlled arms are also a hazard; survive the song while WHRNNNG and THUD-THUD wall off the room. His captions are the clock: [Well. I guess my arms belong to the goo now.] [Tempo's good, though. Very steady.] [Ride cymbal sounds a bit tinny. Should probably buy a new one if I survive this.]
13. Halt. Slime drips off the ceiling. Lucky: "Holy shit. You're a machine." Ryan: [I am literally a hostage.] / "So, do I get the gig?" Bajonka drops a multi-page legally binding band agreement on the snare. Ryan joins.
14. *ONE MONTH LATER (duct-taped card, Toon 0).* Brian plays soulless chords standing still. Ryan: [I give this guy three weeks. Tops.]
15. *(Toon 2)* Chorus. KRAAAAANG manifests; the G swings like a bat and knocks Brian's boutique iced coffee onto the pedalboard. Sparks. "I can't work like this! I hate this weird, creative shit! Can't we just play normal music?!"
16. *ONE MONTH BEFORE THE POTATO BELT TOUR.* Brian pacing: "not here to have fun," "not into this creative shit," "wasting his time." Lucky unplugs — POP — slime bristling like a cat. Phoenix silences the bass and steps back.
17. Lucky, shockingly calm: "Listen to me very carefully, Brian." "You need to stop being an asshole, or you are fired." "We clearly have some fundamental differences in how we think a band should work, but that's something we can discuss. What I can't do is work with an asshole, so whatever's going on at home or wherever, don't bring it to rehearsal."
18. X-ray of Brian's brain; gears spin backward. "Did you just say my foundation is broken?! Are you calling my house crooked?!" Background slime freezes mid-drip. Phoenix: "My brain thinks you might be having a stroke."
19. Lucky: "I literally didn't say anything about a house..." Brian: "I know what you meant! My property values are fine! You're just jealous of my equity!" Door slam. Ryan: [Well. His timing was terrible anyway.] Brian leaves; the FRIED PEDALBOARD stays as a key item.
20. Bajonka drops a crumpled flyer, sneezes, leaves. "GIG TONIGHT: PALMER'S BAR – NO GUITAR SOLO NECESSARY." Phoenix: "My brain thinks she's making a point."
21. *Palmer's (Toon 1).* Six-inch stage. Ryan is already a bubbling mass of green. In the crowd: pristine leather jacket, price tag on, glued mohawk. "Dispatch, I have located the unauthorized slime gathering. Stand by."
22. No Brian to ruin the mix. The first power chord blows the front row's hair back; the mohawk flies into a stranger's beer. (The stranger is tall and says "whoa, man.") Ryan: [No obnoxious guitar solos. Just pure, unadulterated rhythm. The slime is pulsating in 7/8 time. I can work with this.]
23. *Boss: THE SET AT PALMER'S (Toon 3→4).* Enemies: the cop's backup and a crowd-hostility meter. A POCKET gauge fills with every on-beat action. Ryan is uncontrollable; at 75%: [I've been fighting the possession. That was a mistake.] He drops his shoulders; green darkens to bruised purple. [I am not trapped in the goo. The goo is trapped in the pocket.]
24. *THE ETERNITY (Toon 4, scripted).* The art ruptures into cosmic horror over a punk show. Ryan dissolves into a purple-black black hole of rhythm; DOOM / CRACK / THUD manifest with gravity. [Rhythm achieved.] No inputs. A letter smashes the ceiling; the roof peels like a sardine can; walls blow outward; the cop sprints off holding his mohawk; the cymbal crash shakes the screen. An unstoppable win — the mirror of Planets Collide.
25. *Rubble (Toon 1).* Lucky: "Okay. That is our sound." Phoenix: "My brain thinks we are officially domestic terrorists." Ryan, solidifying, holding two intact sticks, aloud for the first time all gig: "I think I broke the space-time continuum on that snare fill."
26. *The quit.* Ryan looks at his hands, then at the sky where the ceiling was. [I could have killed every person in this room.] Aloud: "I quit." Lucky goes magenta in one frame. "We just found it. We just FOUND it—" Dialogue choice: rage / plead / joke. Every branch ends the same: "Yep," and he walks north. Phoenix: "My brain thinks he's scared." Lucky: "I don't care what he is."
27. *Stinger.* Bajonka trots from under drywall, van keys in her mouth, itinerary under her collar: POTATO BELT: TOMORROW circled in red. Lucky, locked magenta: "We don't have a drummer." Bajonka sneezes. NEXT: THE POTATO BELT.

**Bosses:** Tutorial drone (color HUD, riff attacks). AUDITION (ally-as-hazard survival; captions as timer). THE SET AT PALMER'S (Pocket gauge; the Eternity as a finisher the player watches, not plays).
**Stinger:** No drummer, a stolen van, a tour tomorrow.

## Midpoint design

Planets Collide is the midpoint because it is the only fight the band's noise cannot win, and it takes away everything Disc 1 accumulated at once: the apartment Phoenix sold a month of silence for, the van, the coupon, Shannon's armchair, and the laser guitar Lucky's whole identity has run through since the pod bay. Palmer's Bar is its mirror — an unstoppable scripted win the player watches; Chapter 6 is an unstoppable scripted loss the player plays. INTERMISSION is the game's one honest lie, and the fade-in on the couch is the first scene where Lucky has nothing to hide behind. The last line of Disc 1 is the first time Lucky leads instead of rages.

Balancing the halves: Issues 2 and 3 become one chapter (the library cliffhanger runs straight into the mansion); Issues 5 and 6 become one (the Embargo is a single held breath); Episode 0 expands into the slice and carries weight the comic treated incidentally — the drummer churn is now a wound with a lie attached, and Diego, Warner and Billy are seeded in it. Six chapters, each turning on a relationship: Phoenix, Brian/Ryan, Diego/Warner, Alibi, the landlord, Kurt. ## Billy Ruben

Billy Ruben is amber slime — bilirubin-yellow — and the other half of Lucky's first band, Jaundice: two teenagers in a Planet California storage unit playing two-guitar noise under a WORK IS LOVE billboard until Lucky's Dad shut the unit down as unmarketable. The escape was planned together. Billy was late; Billy was always late. Dad's intercom started talking, Lucky slapped it and hit EJECT alone, and has spent the whole story telling themself Billy didn't show. The Chapter 8 tape says otherwise: Billy walked into the bay as the pod fired, stood under the contrail, and spent a year building a van to follow it. The trail was never hard — a contrail over Minneapolis, a pothole, a leveled bar, a trench of potato slime, a shattered dome, a gravity leak in a garage where a drummer said "Yep" and got in the van.

The Han Solo moment is Chapter 11, Phase 2, and nowhere earlier. It is the literal trench run: the final boss, the hero's weapon gone, the villain naming the wound aloud — then the hull breaches. By then Lucky has earned it: they led the scavenger run with only their voice, signed the Eclipse paperwork for the crew, sat across from Dad without going beige, and drove to Ryan's garage for a fight and left with a cymbal. The rescue rewards growth instead of replacing it. It answers both halves of the wound in one van — Billy, whom Lucky abandoned, driving; Ryan, who abandoned Lucky, on the kit — because Lucky's rage at Ryan was always partly guilt about Billy. And the laser guitar returns at the moment Lucky no longer needs it to be whole, which is why Lucky can put it down thirty seconds later and talk to the camera. Post-INTERMISSION would have undercut "Get the synth in the pod"; Ryan's garage would have made Ryan's choice about Billy instead of about Ryan. The Chapter 9 stinger gives the player the Han-is-coming dread without giving Lucky the comfort.

## Ending variants

**A. The Collage (closest to the comic).** Lucky drops the laser guitar and talks to the player: "An orange elephant? Really? We lost our apartment for a first-year improv level allegory? This is embarrassing for everyone involved." The critique breaks the engine: Trunk's sprite shatters and is replaced by a digitized photo-collage sprite (pre-rendered texture against pixel art) on a blank white field; the battle menu corrupts into a text editor. *Mechanics:* no command works except a new one, ROLL. Lucky reaches into the frame, rolls the collage into a joint, lights it on the plasma strings, exhales pink smoke. *Last beat:* the fuel gauge, Artisanal Anti-Matter, coordinates for the next gig.

**B. The Setlist (player-native).** The wall Lucky breaks is the game's: "You've been pressing the same button for forty hours. An elephant?" Trunk's power is attention; every attack feeds his engagement meter. *Mechanics:* the only winning input is a new command, STOP PLAYING. The battle UI closes; the band stands on a yacht with a very loud, suddenly very small elephant; Phoenix's song plays in full as they ignore him and he deflates over the credits. *Last beat:* identical — still broke, next gig.

**C. The Encore (two drummers).** Phase 3 is the finale: Ryan lets go into The Eternity on purpose, Sticky's 13/8 rides inside the black hole's pocket, two guitars double the storage-unit riff, Phoenix's song is the melody. *Mechanics:* a Wall of Sound gauge replaces damage; tweet-birds, NDAs and paywall convert to feedback as it climbs; at 100% the yacht's gravity tears the throne room open. Lucky's fourth-wall break is one line over the noise — "This part's for us." — and turning their back on Trunk is the hit that ends him. *Last beat:* same cockpit; Ryan in the back seat: [Tempo's good, though.]
