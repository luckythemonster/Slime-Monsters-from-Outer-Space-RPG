# Slime Monsters from Outer Space — Story Structure Bible (JRPG build, structure-first lens)

Stance: the comic is a tour diary; the game needs a spine. The spine is **who leaves the room, and who comes back.** Lucky hits EJECT on Billy (Ch 1, unseen), Ryan hits EJECT on Lucky (Ch 1, seen), and Disc 2 is Lucky learning they are Ryan. The midpoint inverts Disc 1's one proven rule (*our noise breaks rooms*) into *their noise breaks our world, and ours can't stop it.*

Pacing: the comic is front-loaded, so Disc 1 compresses nine scripts into six chapters (Issues 2+3 one night; 5+6 one embargo) and Disc 2 gets six new chapters, ~9 h per disc.

## Chapter outline

## Midpoint design

Planets Collide inverts the game's one proven rule. Disc 1 teaches, five times, that the band's noise breaks rooms: Palmer's roof, the silo, the dome, the judges' table, the Panopticon's silence. The midpoint offers a sixth room — the sky — and the player, holding every tool the game has given them, cannot move the bar. Ch 1's boss could not be *not* won (LET GO was the only input); Ch 6's cannot be won (every input works; nothing matters). Same fight, opposite sides, INTERMISSION between.

What flips: **home** (eviction threats → no planet); **the instrument** (RIFF locked by contract in Ch 5 → gone in Ch 7, answered by Lucky's voice); **Phoenix** (bass → synth; puddle → choosing when to go Cyan); **the Vanguard** (khakis → the band is wanted); **Lucky** (reactive magenta → leading, in magenta, by choice).

| Ch 1: the universe's signal recruits a drummer who runs | Ch 10: Bajonka's flyer recruits a guitarist who comes |

## Billy Ruben

**Who:** Lucky's bandmate from Planet California — a jaundice-yellow slime (bilirubin), rhythm guitarist, and author of the only rule the band ever had: no guitar solos. Billy holds walls of sound; Lucky writes the songs that stand on them. Their band, the Bile Ducts, played the one un-gentrified bar on the planet.

**Backstory:** there were two pods. Lucky hit EJECT without checking the next one. Billy was in it and watched the contrail through glass. Billy did not chase; Billy took a residency at the same bar and kept playing Lucky's songs. Seeded in Ch 1 (intercom, "Billy's rule"), Ch 9 ("You didn't call, either") and Ch 10 (the refusal).

**The Han Solo moment:** Chapter 12, end of Phase 2 — party wiped, Lucky alone at 1 HP, birds diving. A riff from outside the hull. Billy's van, Bajonka driving (she handed Billy the flyer in Ch 10), comes through the wall in a wall of sound that shreds the birds, and Billy drops into the fifth slot mid-battle. "You hit eject. I'm hitting it back."

**Why there:** (1) The Ch 10 refusal is the hangar scene; without a "no," the arrival is a cameo. (2) Phase 3 requires Lucky to stop playing; someone must hold the wall of sound while they put the guitar down, and that was Billy's only job in the Bile Ducts — characterization, not deus ex machina. (3) It answers Ryan: the band never gets its drummer back; it gets forgiven by the person Lucky left. Disc 2 says "because they suck"; the finale answers with someone who came anyway. (4) The rhythm-section wound is healed by a rhythm guitarist; Ryan's Kit stays in the bag.

## Ending variants

**V1 — Palette Break (closest to the comic).** SAY IT tears the battle UI. Trunk's 16-color sprite shatters; behind it is a deliberately *wrong* asset — a hi-res, photo-textured collage that breaks the game's palette rule — on flat white. Lucky's hand becomes the menu cursor and the player drags it to roll the collage into a joint; the only menu is SMOKE? → YES. **Mechanics:** one command, one drag input; Billy's Wall of Sound holds the battle open until the drag completes. **Last beat:** the cockpit, anti-matter, "sell some merch." (Collage subject: see questions.)

**V2 — Allegory: OFF.** Lucky's complaint opens the game's own Options menu over the fight. A new toggle sits at the bottom: ALLEGORY [ON]. OFF strips Trunk to a magenta-and-black checkerboard placeholder labeled RICH GUY (TEMP). Lucky rolls the placeholder. **Mechanics:** Phase 3 is a menu puzzle, seeded by an empty last row on every options screen. **Last beat:** the checkerboard cube bungeed to the hood; anti-matter; Diego: "Honestly the placeholder had a better aura."

**V3 — The Scene.** Lucky turns to the crowd, and Billy's van is a clown car: the cyborg farmers, the Librarian (one SHHHH deletes every bird), Greg, Kevin, the Tall Guitarist's finger.
