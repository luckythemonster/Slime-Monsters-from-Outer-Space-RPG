# Slime Monsters from Outer Space — Story Structure Bible (JRPG Build, structure-first lens)

Editorial stance: the comic is a tour diary; the game needs a spine. The spine is **"who leaves the room, and who comes back"** — Lucky hits EJECT on Billy (Ch 1, unseen), Ryan hits EJECT on Lucky (Ch 1, seen), and Disc 2 is Lucky finding out they are Ryan. Every chapter ends broke and ends on a hook; the midpoint flips the one rule Disc 1 teaches (*our noise breaks rooms*) into its inverse (*their noise breaks our world, and ours can't stop it*).

Pacing call: the comic's eight issues are front-loaded, so Disc 1 compresses nine scripts into six chapters (Issues 2+3 merge into one 24-hour night; Issues 5+6 merge into one embargo), and Disc 2 gets six new chapters of the same playtime. Target ~9–10 h per disc.

## Chapter outline

### Chapter 1 — Coagulation (VERTICAL SLICE)

**Locations:** Vanguard star-cruiser pod bay; Minneapolis sky (free-fall); alley behind the dive bar; Phoenix's apartment; Brian's Eden Prairie garage and Ryan's northern garage (cutscenes); City Sound Rehearsal (hallway, Room 4); Palmer's Bar (exterior, stage, rubble). ~2 h.
**Party:** Lucky → +Phoenix → +Brian (temp) → +Ryan (temp) → −Brian → −Ryan. Bajonka attaches at City Sound, never controllable.

**Beats**
1. *Pod bay (Toon 1).* Lucky is a compressed ball of slime. Dad's intercom balloons physically crowd the screen: "I just don't understand the whole nonbinary thing. Why can't you pick a tangible demographic to market yourself to? If you'd just join The Vanguard, you could be financially successful and oppress people with a dental plan!" One seed line added: "And I know you're running off after that Ruben kid." The only interactable is the speaker.
2. *(Toon 3)* The player's first input: a pseudopod slaps the speaker — SQUELCH — cutting Dad mid-balloon. The unnecessarily red EJECT. The pod shreds; free-fall minigame: Lucky flattens like a flying squirrel, slime sunglasses form at top speed, and the pink-and-green contrail is steered through WORK IS LOVE billboards (each one clipped glitches). Twilight Minneapolis.
3. *Alley (Toon 2).* Phoenix, hoodie, trash bag, burnt out. KRATHOOM. The physical CRASH knocks over the dumpsters. Phoenix pushes up their glasses: "My brain thinks you're going to have to pay the city for that pothole."
4. *Tutorial battle: THE CRASH.* The letters C-R-A-S-H get up and rampage. Lucky fights with Slime Slap (the intercom move); Phoenix swings the trash bag. The H breaks a dumpster open: Lucky's guitar case fell separately. Equip → the RIFF command unlocks. Teaches that Lucky's palette tracks HP/mood (magenta = more damage, less accuracy).
5. Lucky pop-pop-pops humanoid and rants (fascist Dad, the Bad Guys, Planet California). Phoenix: college, collapse, rent, trapped in the Midwest. A shared aura links them. "I want to make the loudest, most obnoxious noise possible until this whole empire gets a migraine." / "Beats the day job." Phoenix joins.
6. *Apartment (Toon 2).* Posters, unpaid bills, an unplugged bass amp. "We need a drummer. I could put a flyer up at the coffee shop..." / "Too slow. I'm going to ask the universe." Slime into the input jack; the soundwave bends the screen border outward.
7. *Signal montage.* PRODUCTIVITY IS FREEDOM glitches; highways; pines; Ryan in the freezing garage, sticks frozen mid-air, eyes wide. Then — new — the wave spills out of every Bluetooth speaker in the metro, including a pristine pedalboard in an Eden Prairie garage. Brian, Zappa shirt: "Hon, somebody posted a demo in the Musicians group." End Part 1.
8. *City Sound hallway (Toon 0–1).* Bajonka outside Room 4 with a clipboard. Nod. Sneeze. Time-skip: the band is inside, the clipboard is gone. Brian is setting up: "Do we really need all the political stickers? We're going to alienate the market." / "Hey, man, can you turn the bass down?" / "I'm not a 'man,' Brian. And no." Brian joins. His kit is a joke that teaches the meter: *Boutique Overdrive* spends the full Pocket meter for 1 damage; *Market Research* reveals enemy HP. Jam battle 1, no drums: the Pocket meter visibly cannot fill. The game shows the hole.
9. Brian: "You need drums. I know a guy up north. Doesn't talk. Very reliable." Cut to Ryan's garage; his phone buzzes: *hey bud. band needs drums. u in?* [Well. Sure.] The universe called; Brian texted.
10. Ryan arrives carrying an impossible armload of hardware and silently tightens a cymbal stand. "You made good time from the transmission. You cool with playing fast?" / "Yep." / "My brain thinks we should have asked his rate first. We have exactly four dollars and a half-ounce of weed." Two stick clicks.
11. *Jam battle 2 (Toon 3).* "One, two, three, four!" On the first downbeat the amp erupts and slime splatters Ryan and the kit; the screen jolts sideways and the gutter tears. Ryan is possessed, arms a multi-limb smear; square captions appear in the HUD: [Well. I guess my arms belong to the goo now.] [Tempo's good, though. Very steady.] [Ride cymbal sounds a bit tinny. Should probably buy a new one if I survive this.] Teaches the Pocket meter filling; Ryan's *Fill* is auto-cast and uncontrollable.
12. Silence; slime drips off the cymbals. "Holy shit. You're a machine." [I am literally a hostage.] "So, do I get the gig?" Bajonka drops the multi-page band agreement on the snare. Ryan joins. Save point.
13. *ONE MONTH LATER* (duct-taped card). Rehearsal montage as three short jam battles. Brian plays sterile chords; each chorus the KRAAAANG manifests (Toon 2 object); in the third the G swings like a bat and knocks his boutique iced coffee over the $2,000 pedalboard. Sparks. "I can't work like this! I hate this weird, creative shit! Can't we just play normal music?!" [I give this guy three weeks. Tops.]
14. *ONE MONTH BEFORE THE POTATO BELT TOUR.* Brian paces, jagged balloons: "not here to have fun," "not into this creative shit," "wasting his time." Lucky unplugs — POP — shoulder slime bristling. Phoenix mutes the bass and steps back.
15. *The ultimatum (Toon 1).* Lucky, shockingly calm: "Listen to me very carefully, Brian." / "You need to stop being an asshole, or you are fired." / "We clearly have some fundamental differences in how we think a band should work, but that's something we can discuss. What I can't do is work with an asshole, so whatever's going on at home or wherever, don't bring it to rehearsal." No dialogue choice is offered: nothing saves Brian.
16. X-ray of Brian's brain; gears spin backward. "Did you just say my foundation is broken?! Are you calling my house crooked?!" The background slime freezes mid-drip. "My brain thinks you might be having a stroke." / "I literally didn't say anything about a house..." / "I know what you meant! My property values are fine! You're just jealous of my equity!" Door slams. [Well. His timing was terrible anyway.] Brian leaves; the pedalboard stays as a sellable item worth $2,000 in a shop that offers $40.
17. Bajonka drops the flyer: "GIG TONIGHT: PALMER'S BAR - NO GUITAR SOLO NECESSARY." Lucky, quietly: "Billy's rule." (Seed.)
18. *Palmer's (Toon 1).* Six-inch stage. Ryan already a bubbling green mass at attention. In the crowd: pristine leather with a price tag and a glued-on mohawk. "Dispatch, I have located the unauthorized slime gathering. Stand by."
19. *BOSS: THE PALMER'S SET.* Nod — no Brian to ruin the mix. First power chord: the shockwave blows the front row's hair back and the mohawk into a stranger's beer. [No obnoxious guitar solos. Just pure, unadulterated rhythm. The slime is pulsating in 7/8 time. I can work with this.] The enemy is the Room: a CROWD meter to win and a STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY bar you do not want to lower. Every strong riff raises one and chips the other. The Pocket meter passes 100% for the first time; the gutters fill with static (Toon 3). A new command appears on Ryan: **LET GO**.
20. [I've been fighting the possession. That was a mistake.] The cursor drifts to LET GO; it is the only input left. Slime darkens to bruised purple. [I am not trapped in the goo. The goo is trapped in the pocket.]
21. *THE ETERNITY (Toon 4).* The art ruptures: palette inverts to purplish-black, the HUD disintegrates, DOOM / CRACK / THUD orbit him with visible gravity. [Rhythm achieved.] Ryan is uncontrollable; every turn maxes the crowd and the building. The roof peels like a sardine can; walls blow outward in slow motion; the cop sprints off clutching his mohawk; a final cymbal crash shakes the screen. The first boss is won by destroying the venue, and the player could not avoid it. (Remember this at the midpoint.)
22. *Rubble (Toon 1).* Plaster dust. "Okay. That is our sound." / "My brain thinks we are officially domestic terrorists." / "I think I broke the space-time continuum on that snare fill."
23. *The quit (new).* Ryan looks at his two intact sticks. [That wasn't the goo.] [That was me.] Out loud: "I'm done." He lays the sticks on the snare in the rubble. Lucky goes magenta in one frame, spikes out. "We found it. You *felt* it. You don't get to feel that and walk." / "Yep." He walks north. "My brain thinks he's going to walk the whole way to Duluth." Ryan leaves the party. The kit stays in the rubble (Bajonka loads it; "Ryan's Kit" sits in the inventory all game).
24. *Stinger.* Bajonka trots out from under the drywall, van keys in her mouth, itinerary under her collar: "POTATO BELT: TOMORROW" circled in red. No drummer. No guitarist. Tour tomorrow. Caption: "NEXT: THE POTATO BELT." Post-credit: the cop, radio: "Dispatch — the asset is pink. Repeat, the asset is pink." Cut to a beige hand on Planet California stamping a memo: ASSET LOCATED.

**Bosses:** The CRASH (SFX as enemy, tutorial); Jam Battles (meter tutorial, Ryan auto-cast); the KRAAANG (gag battle with a scripted coffee ending); The Palmer's Set / The Eternity (Toon 4 rupture, the building is the casualty).
**Stinger:** van keys, tour tomorrow, two empty slots, and the Vanguard knows.

## Midpoint design

Planets Collide is the true reversal because it inverts the game's one proven rule. Disc 1 teaches, six times, that the band's noise breaks rooms: Palmer's roof, the silo beams, the mansion dome, the paywall of a judges' table, the Panopticon's silence. The midpoint presents a seventh room — the sky — and the player, with every tool the game has handed them, cannot move the bar. Ch 1's boss could not be *not* won (LET GO was the only input, the venue died regardless); Ch 6's boss cannot be won (every input works, nothing matters). Those two fights are the same fight from opposite sides, and the INTERMISSION card sits between them.

What flips at the intermission: **home** (eviction threats → no planet); **the instrument** (RIFF locked by contract in Ch 5 → RIFF gone in Ch 7, and the answer is Lucky's own voice); **Phoenix** (bass to synth; anxiety-puddle to a player who chooses when to go Cyan); **Laserstein** (dinner-party creep → planet-killer → corpse, a villain who escalates by disappearing); **the Vanguard** (a nuisance in khakis → the band is wanted); **the vehicle** (a stolen van → the boss who beat them); **Lucky** (reactive and magenta → leading, by choice, in magenta).

## Billy Ruben

**Who:** Lucky's bandmate from Planet California — a jaundice-yellow slime (bilirubin; the joke pays off when Billy "goes orange" with excitement), rhythm guitarist and the owner of the only rule the band ever had: no guitar solos. Billy holds walls of sound; Lucky writes the songs that stand on them. Their old band, the Bile Ducts, played the one un-gentrified bar on the planet, directly under a WORK IS LOVE billboard.

**Backstory:** the night of the escape there were two pods. Lucky hit EJECT without checking the next one. Billy was in it, and watched the contrail from behind glass. Billy did not chase. Billy took a residency at the same bar and kept playing Lucky's songs with a drum machine where Lucky used to stand. The game seeds this four times: Dad's intercom line in Ch 1; "Billy's rule" on the Palmer's flyer; Shannon's "You didn't call, either" in Ch 9; the refusal in Ch 10, where Billy is not angry, which is worse.

**The Han Solo moment:** Chapter 12, end of Phase 2. The party is wiped, Lucky is alone at 1 HP, the tweet-birds are diving. A riff from outside the hull. Billy's van — Bajonka driving, because the Ch 10 stinger was her handing Billy the flyer — comes through the wall with a wall of sound that shreds the birds; Billy drops into the fifth party slot mid-battle. "You hit eject. I'm hitting it back."

**Why there and not earlier:** (1) The refusal in Ch 10 is the hangar scene; without a "no," the arrival is a cameo. (2) Mechanically, Phase 3 requires Lucky to stop playing — the fourth-wall break only works in silence from Lucky, and someone has to hold the wall of sound while they put the guitar down. That is literally Billy's only job in the Bile Ducts, so the rescue is characterization, not a deus ex machina. (3) Structurally it answers Ryan: the band never gets its drummer back; it gets forgiven by the person Lucky left. The player has spent Disc 2 absorbing "because they suck"; the finale replies with someone who came anyway. (4) The rhythm-section wound (drums) is healed by a rhythm guitarist — the kit in the rubble stays in the inventory to the end.

## Ending variants

**V1 — Palette Break (closest to the comic).** Phase 3: Lucky's SAY IT tears the battle UI. Trunk's 16-color sprite shatters like glass, and behind it is a deliberately *wrong* asset: a hi-res, dithered, photo-textured collage that violates the game's palette rule on purpose. The background goes to flat white. Lucky's hand becomes the menu cursor, and the player drags it to roll the collage into a joint; the only menu is SMOKE? → YES. Pink smoke clears to the yacht. **Mechanics:** Phase 3 has one command and one drag input; Billy's Wall of Sound ticks in the background so the "battle" never technically ends until the drag completes. **Last beat:** the cockpit, anti-matter, "sell some merch." Requires a decision on what the collage depicts (see questions).

**V2 — Allegory: OFF.** Lucky's complaint opens the game's own Options menu over the fight. A new toggle sits at the bottom: ALLEGORY [ON]. Setting it OFF strips Trunk to what the developers never bothered to draw: a placeholder sprite, magenta-and-black checkerboard, labeled RICH GUY (TEMP). Lucky rolls the placeholder. **Mechanics:** Phase 3 is a menu puzzle (the toggle is only findable because every options screen all game had an empty last row). **Last beat:** the checkerboard cube bungeed to the hood; anti-matter; Diego: "Honestly the placeholder had a better aura."

**V3 — The Scene.** Lucky turns to the crowd instead of the camera, and Billy's van turns out to be a clown car: the cyborg farmers, the Librarian (one SHHHH deletes every bird), Greg, Kevin, Kurt's hand through the hull, the Tall Guitarist's single finger. The final command is EVERYONE; the elephant is rolled by a hundred hands and the smoke spells NOISE IS LOVE across the sky where WORK IS LOVE used to be. **Mechanics:** every chapter's CROWD meter has been secretly banked; Phase 3 damage is that total, so the ending is proportional to how well the player played the whole tour. **Last beat:** the cockpit, anti-matter — "My brain thinks we can charge them for the ride." Softer on the fourth wall; strongest thematically.

All three keep the staged suicide, Trunk, paywall/toilet/birds, Billy's arrival, the hood ornament and the fuel gauge. Recommended default: V1 for fidelity, with V3's banked-crowd damage as the mechanic under it.

## Running gags, motifs, themes

- **Bajonka handled it.** Contracts, van keys, Warner, turtlenecks, the escape pod, the flyer. Each stinger she appears in is the next chapter's premise.
- **Every chapter ends broke.** Pothole bill, eviction warning, EVICTION FINALIZED, new 30-day clock, the deposit, Artisanal Anti-Matter. The economy is the real antagonist; Trunk is just its face.
- **"NO GUITAR SOLO NECESSARY."** Billy's rule, Bajonka's flyer, the prison gig.
- **"Is that a dog?"** A gag until it is the plan.
- **The clipboard.** Slapped away (Ch 2), signed blind (Ch 3), weaponized (Ch 5), read (Ch 9). Lucky's growth is literally paperwork.
- **SFX as objects.** CRASH is a tutorial enemy; KRAAANG knocks over a coffee; DOOM has gravity; T&C clauses are bricks. Toon Level is a gameplay dial.
- **13/8.** A defect, a win, a loaf of challah, an assassination, an earthquake.
- **Color is state.** Lucky's palette variant is the only health bar that matters emotionally; Phoenix's Cyan is a skill with a cost.
- **The pocket.** [The goo is trapped in the pocket.] Disc 2's rematch is won by waiting for the beat.
- **Themes:** noise vs. structure (and learning structure without losing noise); leaving vs. coming back; WORK IS LOVE inverted — the band's work is love and never pays.
