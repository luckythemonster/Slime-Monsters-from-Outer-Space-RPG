# Slime Monsters from Outer Space — JRPG Story Bible (Set-Piece Lens)

A SNES-style JRPG (early FF / Chrono Trigger / Earthbound) in twelve chapters on two "discs." Every chapter owns one unforgettable map, one gimmick boss, and at least one piece of comedy the player *does* rather than reads. Battles are gigs: the shared party meter is the **KRAAANG meter**; filling it raises the fight's **Toon Level (0–4)**, and at Toon 4 the art ruptures. Sound-effect words are physical objects in this universe, so a full meter summons one as a limit break.

## Chapter outline

### Chapter 1 — Coagulation (VERTICAL SLICE)

**Locations:** Vanguard star-cruiser drop-pod bay (Planet California orbit) · Freefall over Minneapolis · Alley behind a dive bar · Phoenix's apartment · Brian's cul-de-sac · Ryan's garage (Northern Minnesota) · City Sound Rehearsal, Room 4 · Palmer's Bar.
**Party:** Lucky → +Phoenix → +Brian → +Ryan (Bajonka joins as field manager at City Sound). Ends: Lucky, Phoenix, Bajonka.

**Beats:**
1. **Title / the intercom (Toon 1).** Cold open inside the pod. Dad's speech balloon fills the screen: *"I just don't understand the whole nonbinary thing. Why can't you pick a tangible demographic to market yourself to? If you'd just join The Vanguard, you could be financially successful and oppress people with a dental plan!"* Tutorial input: hold-and-release to shoot a pseudopod that **slaps the balloon mid-sentence** (SQUELCH). This "cut off a balloon" verb returns as the Ch9 boss mechanic. Hit the unnecessarily red EJECT.
2. **Freefall minigame (Toon 3, `Freefall` scene).** Pod breaks apart; Lucky flattens into a slime flying squirrel. Steer the contrail through "WORK IS LOVE" billboards; each one glitches for a point of *Obnoxiousness*. Max speed forms spiky slime-sunglasses. Target: the alley.
3. **The pothole (Toon 2).** KRATHOOM. Dumpsters fall. Phoenix doesn't flinch. *"My brain thinks you're going to have to pay the city for that pothole."* Key item acquired: **POTHOLE INVOICE — CITY OF MINNEAPOLIS — $1,400**. It is never payable and never leaves the inventory.
4. **Coagulation & grievances (Toon 1).** Pop-pop-pop into humanoid shape. Rant tree: three complaint choices about Dad / the Vanguard / Planet California, each answered by Phoenix's matching Minnesota grievance (college, collapse, rent). Shared aura flickers. *"I want to make the loudest, most obnoxious noise possible until this whole empire gets a migraine."* / *"Beats the day job."* Phoenix joins.
5. **The cosmic Craigslist ad (Toon 2).** Apartment map: posters, unpaid bills, four dollars, a half-ounce. *"We need a drummer. I could put a flyer up at the coffee shop..."* / *"Too slow. I'm going to ask the universe."* Charge minigame: hold to pour slime into the amp jack; at full charge the UI border bends outward. Montage: billboard glitches "PRODUCTIVITY IS FREEDOM," wave rolls north, **Ryan freezes in his garage, sticks hovering.** Phoenix puts up the coffee-shop flyer anyway. "Just in case."
6. **Brian (Toon 0).** Next morning the flyer, not the cosmos, delivers: Brian at the door in a Frank Zappa shirt with a pedalboard the size of a door. *"Your ad said drummer. I play guitar. But I have a pedalboard."* Choice: let him in / don't. Either way Phoenix: "We have four dollars." Brian joins (2nd). His battle commands: **Normal Chord** (1 damage, builds no meter) and **Turn the Bass Down** (debuffs Phoenix; Phoenix: *"I'm not a 'man,' Brian. And no."*).
7. **Brian knows a drummer.** "Ryan. My cousin. Best drummer in Minnesota. He will never, ever answer a text." Road trip in Brian's Subaru (short map: pine forest, snow, a detached garage). Ryan is still frozen from the wave. Choice menu for Lucky — every cosmic pitch makes Ryan's caption colder ([Weird.] [Very weird.]). Brian: "It's forty bucks and gas money." Ryan: *"Yep."* Caption: [Brian drove four hours to say one sentence. Respect.] Brian got him in the room.
8. **City Sound, Room 4 (Toon 0–1).** Sticker-plastered hallway. Ryan arrives carrying a physically impossible amount of hardware. Bajonka sits outside the door with a clipboard. Ryan nods; Bajonka sneezes; smash cut — door open, clipboard gone. Nobody asks. *"You made good time from the transmission. You cool with playing fast?"* / *"Yep."* / *"My brain thinks we should have asked his rate first. We have exactly four dollars and a half-ounce of weed."*
9. **TUTORIAL BATTLE — "Room 4" (Toon 3).** Enemy: THE SONG (a target with a tempo bar). Count-off prompt: *"One, two, three, four!"* `onRound(1)`: first downbeat triggers the eruption — pink/green slime splatters Ryan; `transform` to possessed sheet; his single attack becomes a multi-hit octopus smear. Captions in rigid squares: [Well. I guess my arms belong to the goo now.] [Tempo's good, though. Very steady.] [Ride cymbal sounds a bit tinny. Should probably buy a new one if I survive this.] Brian stands perfectly still doing 1 damage. Win: *"Holy shit. You're a machine."* / [I am literally a hostage.] / *"So, do I get the gig?"* Bajonka drops a multi-page band agreement on the snare and sits. Ryan joins; Bajonka joins as field manager.
10. **ONE MONTH LATER (duct-taped card).** Brian's grievance tour: *"Do we really need all the political stickers? We're going to alienate the market."* [I give this guy three weeks. Tops.] Mini-battle "Chorus": the first time the meter fills, the summoned **KRAAANG** manifests and its G swings like a bat into Brian's boutique iced coffee → the $2,000 pedalboard sparks and dies. *"I can't work like this! I hate this weird, creative shit! Can't we just play normal music?!"*
11. **The ultimatum / THE MISINTERPRETATION (playable comedy).** "ONE MONTH BEFORE THE POTATO BELT TOUR." Lucky unplugs with a POP; slime bristles like a cat. Choice menu, three calm, reasonable lines — *"You need to stop being an asshole, or you are fired."* / "We clearly have some fundamental differences in how we think a band should work…" / "…whatever's going on at home or wherever, don't bring it to rehearsal." Pick any. X-ray cutaway of Brian's gears spinning backward. All three resolve to: *"Did you just say my foundation is broken?! Are you calling my house crooked?!"* Background slime freezes mid-drip. *"My brain thinks you might be having a stroke."* / *"I literally didn't say anything about a house..."* / *"I know what you meant! My property values are fine! You're just jealous of my equity!"* Door slams. [Well. His timing was terrible anyway.] Brian leaves the party; his pedalboard stays as a sellable item (worth $12 — fried). Bajonka drops the flyer: **GIG TONIGHT: PALMER'S BAR – NO GUITAR SOLO NECESSARY.**
12. **Palmer's Bar, pre-show (Toon 1).** Free-roam NPC comedy: Warner is finishing a Rat Fight opener and dry-heaving in the bathroom; a very tall guy by the stage says the ceiling "has a heavy aura, man" (Diego, seeded); a man with a price tag on his leather jacket and a glued-on mohawk. Ask him if he's a cop: "Dispatch, I— no." *"Dispatch, I have located the unauthorized slime gathering. Stand by."*
13. **BOSS GIG — "PALMER'S BAR" (Toon 2→4).** See Boss.
14. **Aftermath (Toon 1).** Plaster dust. *"Okay. That is our sound."* / *"My brain thinks we are officially domestic terrorists."* / *"I think I broke the space-time continuum on that snare fill."* Then the new beat: Ryan looks at his hands and they are still vibrating. Out loud, second sentence of the night: "I'm done." He packs the impossible hardware and walks into the snow. Choice menu for Lucky — three responses, all magenta, all wrong (*"We finally found it and you RUN?"*); the color won't come down no matter what you pick. Ryan leaves the party.
15. **Stinger.** Bajonka trots out from under drywall with the van keys and an itinerary: **POTATO BELT: TOMORROW** circled in red. Behind her, the only two people who didn't flee: Diego ("That was the heaviest thing I have ever felt, man") and Warner, holding his own sticks: "I know a drummer." Caption: NEXT: THE POTATO BELT.

**Boss — PALMER'S BAR (Structural Integrity 100%).** The venue is the enemy and you *want* to kill it. Every riff damages Structural Integrity; the undercover cop is a second target who radios dispatch each round he survives (a **Dispatch** bar that, if filled, ends the gig in a raid: soft-fail, retry). The meter fills fast as a three-piece (no Brian to ruin the mix — the dev note is literal: his absence is a damage bonus). `onRound(3)`: a rigid caption — [I've been fighting the possession. That was a mistake.] — and the only player choice given to Ryan all game: **[Keep fighting it] / [Let go].** Fighting it twice makes the crowd thin and Dispatch tick; **Let go** triggers Toon 4: battle backdrop replaced by a painted cosmic-horror plate, UI melts, Ryan's sprite dissolves into **The Eternity**, and his attacks become gravity-weighted SFX words (DOOM, CRACK, THUD) that strike the ceiling. [I am not trapped in the goo. The goo is trapped in the pocket.] [Rhythm achieved.] Integrity hits 0: roof peels like a sardine can, walls blow outward, the mohawk lands in a stranger's beer. The player chose "let go" — and Ryan pays for it. That is why the quitting stings.

**Stinger:** van keys, itinerary, two strangers in the rubble.

### Chapter 2 — Gig in the Potato Belt

**Locations:** The space-van (overworld vehicle; a late-90s passenger van with welded thrusters) · The Potato Belt (radioactive crop rows under hovering combine-fortresses) · Underground Barn, Sector 4 · The loading dock.
**Party:** Lucky, Phoenix, Diego (joins), Warner (temp). Bajonka field.

**Beats:** 1. Opening splash: the least aerodynamic spacecraft in the universe. Diego: *"Man, the stars out here are heavy."* Phoenix pilots: *"My brain thinks if I have to manually parallel park this thing in zero gravity, I am going to walk into the sun."* Parking minigame: three attempts, each worse. 2. Diego's phone in the van: Ryan posted that he quit "because they suck." Lucky goes magenta; thrusters surge; the van gains a speed boost whenever Lucky is enraged (a running overworld perk). 3. Landing SQUELCH. Humidity status effect: Phoenix **Melting** (loses HP per step outside; *"Put me in a Tupperware container."*). 4. Load-in. Worker with a welding-torch arm: *"I am going to bite someone."* 5. Khaki feds: *"They look like they're about to audit a mosh pit."* Diego's visa panic; *"Diego. If you send a cop a 'positive vibration,' I will personally launch you into the sun."* 6. The set: "Monsters." Lucky's leg stretches across stage to tweak a knob (slime-physics riff unlock). The agent IDs "the missing Vanguard biological asset." 7. Breach. 8. Loading dock cutaway: *"Hey... is that a dog?"* THUD. Bajonka drags a 100-lb cab up the ramp. 9. Promoter: *"There is no door money. The gig was a stress test. And you passed."* The Eclipse clipboard. *"I'm not joining your bureaucratic book club!"* Sack of RAW COSMIC POTATOES taken as payment.

**Boss — VANGUARD RIOT DRONES ×3 ("The Riot Solo").** Rhythm boss: Warner's crash cymbal flashes on a beat; Lucky's guitar-bat swing only crits if the input lands on the crash. Diego starts **Hiding** behind the bass cab (unusable; *"Whoa. Bad vibes. No visa."*) and Phoenix starts **Confused** (*"My brain thinks we missed a time signature change."*) — you win round 1 with one guitarist and one drummer, then rally them back by keeping the beat. Diego, from behind the amp: *"That was actually a really nice phrasing, man."*

**Stinger:** Diego eats a raw potato. Phoenix's phone: EVICTION WARNING. *"My brain thinks we need to start selling merch."*

### Chapter 3 — The Merch Table Incident

**Locations:** The Earth-Remnant Library (asteroid station): circulation desk, reading-area pit, Young Adult Fiction merch table, Sci-Fi/Fantasy aisle, green room.
**Party:** Lucky, Phoenix, Diego, Warner (temp; starts at 1 HP after the Rat Fight set).

**Beats:** 1. *"I kind of love this room. The acoustics smell like old paper."* Rat Fight ends; *"SHHHHHHHH!"* / *"Woo."* Library rule: the **Noise meter** — any loud riff in this map triggers a Shush that silences the party for a turn. 2. Steven Alibi's case cracks the floorboards. *"I know who you are. The biological anomaly band."* The critique lands on every insecurity — and he adds: "I ran into your drummer. He said he left because you suck. I found it hard to argue." Lucky: bristling magenta. 3. Merch: Sharpie potatoes. *"We are all slime."* / *"Rice is a more desirable side dish."* Diego's **Talk** command unlocked here, on vegetables. *"He used a permanent marker. The ink burns."* 4. The set is sloppy; Alibi shakes his head and leaves; Lucky deflates to pale pink. *"My brain thinks we are the worst band in this sector."* 5. Diego flips the table. 6. *"Get this garbage out of my way before I start my set."* EVICTION FINALIZED. 7. The promoter's briefcase: an Eclipse officer is on tonight's menu. *"Cannibalism is fundamentally un-chill."* Lucky signs without reading.

**Boss — THE GREAT TUBER (Insecurity 100%).** The scattered potatoes, irradiated and humiliated, coalesce into one lumpy telepathic spud. Attacking it makes it *bigger* (it internalizes). Only Diego's Talk tree lowers Insecurity: *"You're a complex starch, man. You have value."* / *"You can be mashed! You can be fried! You are versatile!"* The rest of the party's job is crowd control under the Shush rule. Win: the Tuber splits into harmless potatoes; one stays in the cup holder forever ("Little Guy").

**Stinger:** *"Buckle up, little guy. We're going to go fight some billionaires."*

Your projection was incredibly weak..."* / *"I was wearing the glasses! I said 'synergy'!"* / *"Fine. Let's do some inner truth. 1, 2, 3, 4!"*

**Boss — THE REINFORCED DOME (Resonance).** Sticky's first fight: his turns arrive on a **13-step grid** the player taps; hitting the odd accents cracks the dome's resonance bar. **Stinger:** Holding breath in the void. Bajonka already has the van airlock open. A mop-dog in a Vanguard security vest floats past, into the dark. Laserstein's yacht is gone. Shannon, gasping: "Decent exit. Work on the entrance."

### Chapter 8 — The Luthier of Debris

**Locations:** The debris field (pod as hub; asteroid hopping) · The shattered Vanguard armory (zero-G interior, drifting ordnance) · The apartment's drifting remains (an osmium drum machine among them).
**Party:** Lucky (no guitar: **Voice** riffs only), Phoenix (synth; Cyan toggle), Diego, Sticky.

**Beats:** 1. Inventory: Lucky's instrument slot reads "—". New verb: **Terms & Conditions** — Lucky screams Vanguard legalese and the balloons solidify as pink blocks. *"VOID WHERE PROHIBITED BY LAW!"* 2. Parts hunt (Sticky's list): osmium neck (one of Alibi's crushed machines, drifting), plasma-jet strings (armory), laser-bomb pickups (armory core), body: the rusted hubcap of the old van. 3. Phoenix learns the synth; Diego's potato survived in his pocket. 4. Sticky welds while the drones come. 5. The hubcap guitar is born. It is ugly. Lucky loves it.

**Boss — THE ARMORY CORE & SALVAGE SWARM.** Text-versus-metal: the swarm "accepts" any clause used twice (immune), so the player assembles ever-longer legalese from a growing word bank for bigger blocks, on a cooldown. The Core detonates ordnance in zero-G that Phoenix's synth pads can freeze. First swing of the hubcap guitar ends it.

**Stinger:** The pod's nav pings Laserstein's yacht transponder. Destination: **Planet California.** Lucky's slime goes a color we haven't seen: pink with a gray edge. Dread.

### Chapter 9 — Planet California

**Locations:** Planet California (gentrified billboard world: every surface sells something) · The underground basement circuit where NO DENTAL used to play · Radio Free California (Billy's pirate station inside a hollowed "WORK IS LOVE" billboard) · Vanguard Lifestyle Tower (Dad's office) · The roof of the tower under the cruiser from screen one.
**Party:** Lucky, Phoenix, Diego, Sticky; +Billy Ruben (guest, end of chapter).

**Beats:** 1. Landing. Everyone recognizes Lucky; nobody recognizes them *kindly*. Phoenix: *"My brain thinks this planet is a mall that ate a mall."* 2. Laserstein is here buying protection from Lucky's Dad. 3. The basement circuit: Lucky's old flyers, their old band NO DENTAL, and the name Billy Ruben scratched into every stage. Lucky refuses to talk about it (a dialogue option greyed out: "— don't."). 4. Billy's broadcast cuts into every billboard: a riff Lucky knows. Choice: go to the station / go to the tower. Both happen; order changes dialogue. 5. The tower: Laserstein's deal collapses when the band crashes the lobby gig; Dad has Laserstein arrested to save face (Laserstein shipped to the Vanguard Maximum Security Space-Prison). 6. Dad's office. 7. The roof: the star-cruiser descends, tractor beam, the planet's power cut so the amps die. 8. **The Han Solo moment** (see Billy Ruben). 9. Lucky's first full chord on the hubcap guitar blows the beam.

**Boss — DAD ("Quarterly Performance Review").** He never leaves his desk. His attacks are intercom speech balloons that *grow* while he talks ("tangible demographic," "dental plan," "your mother and I"); each must be slapped mid-sentence with the Chapter 1 pseudopod timing or it lands as full damage. Dad heals when the party argues (choosing a rebuttal line restores his HP — the only way to win is to stop engaging and play). Bajonka, meanwhile, is quietly shredding his filing cabinet.

**Stinger:** Billy's billboard-barge pulls the band aboard. Billy to Lucky, before anything else: "You left the amp on, dude. I could hear you from here."

### Chapter 10 — Because They Suck

**Locations:** Earth, Minnesota, deep winter · Palmer's Bar II (rebuilt by the Vanguard as an "authentic dive experience"; the roof is a tarp) · The north woods, fenced "ANOMALY — DO NOT PLAY" · Ryan's garage, now a localized black hole.

## Midpoint design

**Why Planets Collide is the hinge.** Disc 1 is about finding a sound and losing the people who make it (Brian, Ryan, Warner, the apartment). Disc 2 is about getting them back (Billy, Ryan, a guitar, a vehicle). The collision wipes the board — guitar, van, home, planet — so Disc 2 can rebuild every system the player learned, slightly different: Voice riffs before a guitar, synth before a bass, a pod before a barge.

**The unwinnable fight, honestly.** Autosave fires on the roof. The HUD is truthful: the player deals real damage numbers, but Kurt's gravity bar is labeled **GRAVITY**, not HP, and it climbs each round regardless. Sticky takes self-damage every turn forcing 13/8 to wait (sparks, grinding SFX); Diego is Reverent for three rounds. Round 4: the yacht hyperspaces (the villain leaving is the tell). Round 6: the second planet fills the backdrop. The last prompt is a single button: **ONE MORE CHORD.** Cut to black, no music, no SFX. **INTERMISSION** in plain white type, then a fake SNES-era card — *PLEASE TURN THE CARTRIDGE OVER* — held for a beat too long. No Game Over screen ever appears; the fade-in to the debris field is the only continue. Retroactively, Lucky names the rule: "We can't out-heavy a planet." The rematch in Chapter 11 reuses the identical HUD so the player feels the difference the moment the gravity bar *stalls*.

**Re-pacing the front half.** Issues 5 and 6 merge into one Embargo chapter; Issue 3's dome shatter becomes a chapter-ending cliffhanger instead of a mid-issue page; Disc 2 is seeded in Disc 1 (NO DENTAL sticker on Lucky's amp in Ch1; "Trunk Fund" tech-bro in Ch4; Kurt's disqualification and the Trunk-subsidiary sponsor in Ch5; Pete's floating brother in Ch4). Estimated playtime: Disc 1 nine hours, Disc 2 eight.

## Billy Ruben

**Who.** A bilirubin-orange slime (sun-bleached, the joke in the name), bassist and wall-of-sound engineer, now the pirate DJ of Radio Free California, broadcasting from inside a hollowed-out "WORK IS LOVE" billboard. Plays an eight-string through a stack of refrigerators; the barge is the amp.

**Backstory.** Lucky and Billy were NO DENTAL, two slimes playing the basement circuit under the billboards. Their biggest show was raided by Vanguard security on Dad's orders; Lucky was dragged onto the star-cruiser — the pod bay on screen one is where that night ends — and Billy was left on the floor. Lucky assumed Billy got arrested, or gave up, or sold out, and has not said the name since. Billy kept the amp. When Lucky "asked the universe" in Chapter 1, the wave reached Planet California; Billy has been following the signal's echoes across the galaxy, one gig behind, ever since.

**The Han Solo moment (Chapter 9, beat 8).** The band is on the roof of the Vanguard Lifestyle Tower. Dad's star-cruiser — the ship from the game's first screen — descends, tractor beam locked on Lucky; Dad has cut the planet's power so the amps are dead; the party's commands grey out one by one. Phoenix: *"My brain thinks this is the part where we get re-podded."* Then every billboard on the planet lights at once and plays the NO DENTAL riff, and a converted billboard-barge drops out of hyperspace, refrigerators glowing, and rakes the cruiser's beam emitter with a wall of sound. The beam stutters. Lucky's hubcap guitar gets power from Billy's broadcast and the player takes the shot: one chord into the emitter. Billy, over the radio, not a pleasantry in sight: "You left the amp on, dude. I could hear you from here."

**Why it lands hardest here.** It is the literal Death Star: the ship Lucky escaped from in the opening, come back for them. The chapter's question is whether Lucky's old life was real or just something they fled, and Billy is the answer — a bandmate who stayed and never held the leaving against them. That forgiveness is the hinge for the next chapter: Billy forgives Lucky for leaving so Lucky can forgive Ryan for leaving. Mechanically it also solves the band's transport problem (the pod is dead; the barge is the Disc 2 overworld vehicle) and gives the finale an encore roster. Placing it at the final boss would steal the band's win; placing it mid-Disc 2 gives the back half its own "I've got you" beat with the finale still theirs.

## Ending variants

All three keep the direction: Laserstein's staged suicide, Trunk on the golden toilet, Paywall and tweet-birds, Lucky's fourth-wall break, the band wins and is still broke. Each ends in the hood-ornament elephant head, the gold cockpit, and *"My brain thinks... my brain thinks it's a hundred thousand credits per gallon."* / *"We better sell some merch at this next one, man."*

**A — Roll Him Up (faithful).** Phase 3: Lucky drops the hubcap guitar. *"An orange elephant? Really? We lost our apartment for a first-year improv level allegory? This is embarrassing for everyone involved."* The game-native collage: the pixel art breaks — Trunk's sprite is replaced by a jagged, JPEG-artifacted photo cutout at the wrong resolution, the battle font flips to Times New Roman, the command menu becomes a gray OS dialog with one button: **ROLL.** Button-mash to roll the collage into a joint; Lucky lights it off the plasma strings; pink smoke. Last beat: Diego unplugs the yacht's right-wing talk radio, Lucky plugs in, coordinates set for the next gig to pay for gas.

**B — Don't Feed the Elephant.** The tweet-birds get stronger the more the party acts; Trunk's bar is labeled ATTENTION. Lucky figures it out mid-fight and turns to the camera: "Everybody stop. He only exists if we keep looking at him." The finisher is to put the controller down: thirteen real seconds (Sticky counts them) with no input while birds scream. Any button restarts the count. At zero the collage glitch happens on its own, Trunk's audience bar empties, and Lucky rolls what's left. Last beat identical, plus Little Guy the potato finally says something secure: "I am a complex starch." Riskiest, most memorable.

**C — The Save File.** Lucky's fourth-wall break opens the game's own pause menu inside the battle. The collage appears as a corrupt save slot: DARNELL_TRUNK.sav (0 KB, 1 billion credits). The dialog: *Are you sure you want to delete this file? This cannot be undone.* Yes. The yacht's cockpit loads as the "new game" screen, elephant head on the hood. Last beat: Dad, downsized by Trunk in Chapter 11, is in the back seat complaining about the demographic of the gig they are flying to; Lucky, for once, lets the balloon finish, then turns the amp up.

## Running gags, motifs, themes

- **Color is the HUD.** Lucky's slime is the mood meter the player reads before the dialogue; magenta also buffs the van's thrusters.
- **"My brain thinks…"** / rigid square Ryan captions / Diego's auras — three voices, three text-box styles (wobbly, square, lowercase).
- **Bajonka's escalation:** clipboard → contract → flyer → van keys → guard → Denim Boys → claymore → dossier → escape pod → playable prison break → the yacht's title paperwork.
- **The Pothole Invoice** rides in the inventory from Chapter 1 to the credits. Phoenix: "My brain thinks they'll find us."
- **Countdowns that are never money:** eviction warning, coupon timer, Day 14/30, 00:00:01, the fuel gauge.
- **Clipboards on both sides.** The Eclipse and the Vanguard are the same bureaucracy with different stickers; Lucky signs anyway when it's for Phoenix.
- **SFX are objects.** KRAAANG knocks over coffee; DOOM breaks a roof; Terms & Conditions are bricks; tweets are birds.
- **Cops in bad disguises** in every crowd, escalating from mohawk to khakis to Intern Unit 74-B to a mop.
- **13/8** as the band's secret: undanceable, unmarketable, and the only thing that shatters paywalls.
- **Letting go vs. holding on.** Ryan lets go and runs; Lucky holds on and rages; Phoenix lets go of "my brain thinks" and becomes something scarier. The count-off is how you forgive.
- **Theme:** creative labor under a fascist corporate state; the empire gets a migraine, and you're still broke.
